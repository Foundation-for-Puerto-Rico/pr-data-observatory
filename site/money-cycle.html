Knowledge Area · Public money

How public money moves

Public money does not go from a budget to a service in one step. It is designed, requested, approved, assigned, committed, carried out and paid, and only then does someone use what it bought. Each step has its own word and its own figure, and a number means little until you know which step it is. This page explains the steps, so the other public money pages can be read with care.

A page that explains. It has no figures of its own. Every statement rests on a law, a rule or an official glossary listed at the end, and the page gives no opinion on how the process should work.

After this page you can

  • Name the stage a figure is in, and say what it does and does not mean.
  • Tell apart approved, obligated, paid and in service.
  • Ask five questions of any figure about public money before drawing a conclusion.

route1 · The stages

The path of the Commonwealth's own budget, from top to bottom. The name of each stage is the word the other pages use.

  1. edit_note
    Design

    An agency works out what it needs the money for and writes its request, with its plan of work.

    The agency
  2. outgoing_mail
    Request

    The Office of Management and Budget puts the requests together into the Governor's proposed budget, which goes to the Oversight Board. The Board approves it or sends it back to be corrected.

    Office of Management and Budget, Governor
  3. how_to_vote
    Approval

    The version the Board approved goes to the Legislature, which evaluates and adopts a budget. The Governor signs it, and the Oversight Board certifies it when it follows the fiscal plan. If it does not, the Board can write the budget itself.

    Legislature, Oversight Board
  4. assignment
    Assignment

    Each agency receives its share of the certified budget for the fiscal year, by fund and by concept of expense.

    Office of Management and Budget
  5. contract
    Obligation

    The agency commits the money: it signs a contract, places an order or hires. From here the government owes the money to someone.

    The agency; purchases go through the General Services Administration
  6. construction
    Execution

    The work is done or the service is given.

    The agency and its contractors
  7. payments
    Spending

    The Treasury pays the bill. Only now is the money spent.

    Department of the Treasury (Hacienda)
  8. verified
    In service

    People use what was paid for: the school is open, the clinic sees patients.

    No one reports it in open data
Three words that are not the same. Approved is what an agency may spend. Obligated is what it has committed by contract. Spent is what has been paid. A year can end with money approved and never obligated, or obligated and not yet paid.

account_balance3 · The federal side

Federal money follows its own path, and it meets the Commonwealth's in the middle. A federal grant is obligated twice: once by the federal agency, when it awards the grant, and again by the recipient here, when it signs the contracts that use it.

  1. Appropriation

    Congress sets aside money for a program.

  2. Award

    A federal agency awards a grant to a recipient in Puerto Rico. For the federal government the money is now obligated.

  3. The recipient commits it

    The recipient, usually a Commonwealth agency, must still buy, contract or pass the money on. A grant can be fully awarded and not yet committed here.

  4. Outlay

    The federal Treasury pays the recipient, often as a refund of what it already spent.

  5. End of the period of performance

    The time to do the work ends. The recipient has 120 days to pay its bills and report.

  6. Closeout

    The federal agency closes the grant. What was not used is taken off the grant (a de-obligation).

Why this matters for the numbers. The federal record (USAspending) shows the award and the outlays. It does not show the second obligation, the one made here, so it cannot say how much of a grant is already under contract.

calendar_month4 · Two calendars

The Commonwealth's fiscal year runs from July to June. The federal one runs from October to September. Both take the name of the year in which they end, so fiscal year 2027 covers different months in each.

JulAugSepOctNovDecJanFebMarAprMayJunJulAugSep
Commonwealth fiscal year
Federal fiscal year

Forty-six states begin their fiscal year on July 1, as Puerto Rico does, so the three-month gap with the federal year is common. It still has a cost: an agency that depends on a federal grant writes its Commonwealth request before it knows what the federal government will give it. On the pages, never compare a Commonwealth figure and a federal figure of the same fiscal year as if they covered the same months.

compare_arrows5 · Services and works move at different speeds

A service

Money for a service, such as child care or job training, is paid as the service is given. Payment and delivery go together, month by month. If little has been paid late in the grant, little has been delivered.

A work

Money for a bridge, a school or a dredging is committed years before the work is finished. It is normal for a large work to show little paid for a long time, and to be in service long after the last payment.

So the same share paid out means different things. Read it next to the kind of program before drawing a conclusion.

help7 · What open data do not show

The last stage has no data. No open source records whether what was paid for is in service: whether the school opened, the clinic sees patients or the office is in use. Paid is the last stage the data reach. A project can be fully paid and not yet serve anyone.
Also missing. The second obligation of federal money, made by the recipient here. Spending as it happens: the Commonwealth's figures of what was spent are what agencies report at the end of a year. And the reasons: why money was not committed, or why an amount was lowered.

difference8 · Often confused

Seven pairs that are easy to read as the same thing. Each one changes what a figure means.

Approved is not spent

A budget gives permission to spend up to an amount. It does not say the money was used. The permission ends with the fiscal year: in the General Fund, money of an earlier year cannot be used unless the certified budget says so. Special funds are different: their income of earlier years can be used, under the rules of each fund.

Obligated is not paid

A signed contract commits the money. The payment comes later, as the work is done and billed.

Paid is not in service

The last bill can be paid before anyone uses what was built. No open source records the opening day.

A balance is not money lost

A balance says an amount had not been paid out on the day the record was read. It does not say why, and it does not say the money is gone.

A reduction is not a payment sent back

A reduction lowers the amount a federal agency had committed. It is a change to money not yet paid out, and the record does not give the reason.

An audit finding is not a verdict

A finding says the auditor saw a rule or a control that was not followed. It can be a late report or a missing record. It does not judge intent.

The same fiscal year is not the same months

The Commonwealth's fiscal year and the federal one share a name and differ by three months.

rule9 · Five questions to ask a figure

Use them on any figure about public money: on these pages, in a report or in the news.

  1. Which stage is it in?

    Approved, obligated, paid or closed. Compare only figures of the same stage.

  2. Whose fiscal year is it?

    July to June for the Commonwealth, October to September for the federal government.

  3. Who reports it, and when was it read?

    An agency, the Treasury, a federal record or an auditor. Records change after the day they are read.

  4. Is it a service or a work?

    A service is paid as it is given. A work is paid over years.

  5. What does it leave out?

    The contracts signed here, whether the result is in use, and the reasons.

quiz11 · Check what you learned

Four cases. Think of your answer, then open ours.

A report says an agency has 90 percent of its budget approved and 40 percent spent with two months left in the year. What can you say, and what can you not?

You can say that less than half of what was approved had been paid. You cannot say how much is under contract, because obligated sits between the two and the figure does not give it. You also cannot say why.

A federal grant for a bridge shows a small share paid out three years after the award. Is that a problem?

The figure alone does not say. A work is paid over years, so a small share is normal early on. You would need the period of performance and the contracts signed here, and the federal record does not show the contracts.

Someone compares the Commonwealth's fiscal year 2027 with the federal fiscal year 2027. What is wrong?

They do not cover the same months. The Commonwealth's year ends in June and the federal one in September.

A school repair is fully paid. Is the school open?

The data cannot say. Paid is the last stage open data reach. In service is the stage no open source records.
