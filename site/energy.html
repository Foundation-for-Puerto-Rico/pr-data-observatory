Energy in Puerto Rico

In 2025, 92.5% of Puerto Rico's electricity came from oil, natural gas and coal. Renewables were 7.5% of production, and more than half of that was rooftop solar sent to the grid (4.8%); the renewable plants alone gave 2.8%. Rooftop systems now add up to 1,622 MW, 4.1 times the 398 MW of renewable power plants, and homes and businesses have 189,459 batteries.

Read with care. Rooftop solar that a home uses itself is not counted as production anywhere: the production figures include only what rooftop systems send to the grid. Capacity (MW) is what equipment is built to produce, not what it produces.

bolt4 · Reliability

SAIDI is the average number of minutes each customer is without power. LUMA's monthly figure counts outages of the transmission and distribution system it runs; outages caused by a lack of generation are not in it.

payments5 · Price

The average residential price: what residential customers paid in the month divided by the electricity they bought, from what utilities report to EIA. It includes the fuel charge, which moves with oil and gas prices.

flood6 · The grid and floods

The transmission lines of the map (OpenStreetMap, simplified) and the power plants (EIA) inside FEMA's 1% annual chance flood area and its coastal high-hazard V zones. The 38 kV network is about half mapped (1,052 km), so its figures are a lower bound.

infoMethod

Production by source. LUMA's monthly net production of the utility's and its contractors' plants by fuel, plus the energy rooftop systems export to the grid. Solar, wind and landfill gas are read from the file's contracted renewable production; the file's second set of columns for these fuels holds other quantities before October 2017. A year is shown when it has its twelve months.
Rooftop solar and batteries. The capacity and the customers registered for net metering in LUMA's distributed generation filing; batteries by municipio from its monthly metrics, where LUMA splits San Juan into San Juan, Río Piedras and Santurce, added back together here.
Floods. The lines and plant points are intersected with the observatory's FEMA flood layer (zones in the 1% annual chance Special Flood Hazard Area; V zones are its coastal high-hazard part). Sea level rise and landslides are not counted yet.
What the page does not say. It does not judge the utility, the operators or the policy, and it does not name any customer, address or installation. The full method and the code are in docs/energy.md of the project repository; each figure has an entry in the data dictionary.
