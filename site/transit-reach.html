Transit in Puerto Rico, barrio by barrio

149 of Puerto Rico's 901 barrios have a bus, train or ferry stop with a current timetable. Another 553 have only service without one: a público route of the 2020 list, a stop whose timetable has ended or a stop mapped by volunteers. 2.0 million people, 62.1% of residents, live in a barrio with no stop with a current timetable; 239,228 of them live where the lists show nothing at all.

What the map is not. It shows what the published lists of routes and timetables hold, not how often a bus comes or whether a público runs today. A barrio without a timetabled stop can still have service, and a barrio with one can have a stop far from most homes.

map1 · The barrios

Each barrio is shaded by the best service the lists put inside it. A yellow edge marks the 52 barrios that a route with a current timetable crosses with no stop, home to 263,445 people. The red lines are the Tren Urbano and the ferries. The second view shades each barrio by the share of households with no vehicle, the people who most depend on transit.

table_view2 · By municipio

In 49 of the 78 municipios no barrio has a stop with a current timetable. 14 municipios report a bus system to the federal National Transit Database but publish no timetable, so their service does not appear on the map: Aguada, Añasco, Barceloneta, Camuy, Carolina, Cataño, Cayey, Comerío, Gurabo, Hormigueros, Lares, Mayagüez, Toa Baja, Yauco. The table is sorted by the residents without a current stop in their barrio; select a heading to sort by another column.

infoHow it is counted

The classes. A barrio has a stop with a current timetable when a stop of ATI (AMA buses, Tren Urbano, ferries) or of a municipal system lies inside it and that system's GTFS timetable has not ended on November 23, 2026. Otherwise it has service without a timetable when the lists put inside it a stop whose timetable has ended, a stop mapped in OpenStreetMap, or a público route of the 2020 list. A stop counts only in the barrio it lies in; a stop just across the line does not count.
Limits of the lists. The público list is from 2020 and has no timetable; a route on it may no longer run. Several municipal timetables have ended or end soon, and a barrio changes class on the day its timetable ends: the map, the figure cards and the table follow the timetables every night, while the text of the page gives the figures of November 23, 2026. 14 municipal systems publish nothing. Dial-a-ride service is not shown: it is reported by agency, not by place. Routes drawn from stop to stop where a system publishes no route shape make the yellow edge approximate.
Residents and households. Residents are those of the 2020 Census, counted by census block. The residents within 400 m of a stop with a current timetable, 732,418 (22.3%), come from the exposure series of the Observatory, computed with the timetables of November 18, 2026. Households with no vehicle are estimates of the American Community Survey 2020-2024 (table B25044) with margins of error; barrios with fewer than 500 residents or a margin as large as the estimate are drawn lighter.
More detail. The code is transit_reach.py in the project repository, and the data file has an entry in the data dictionary (search for 'transit by barrio'). The idea comes from a county map of public transit in the United States made for the 30 Day Map Challenge.

menu_bookSources and licenses

Timetables. Autoridad de Transporte Integrado (ATI), GTFS feed; the municipal systems' GTFS feeds published through the National RTAP portal.
Públicos. The list of público routes of the Autoridad de Carreteras y Transportación, published by the Instituto de Estadísticas de Puerto Rico (2020-03-19), CC BY.
Other stops. OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL; the Federal Transit Administration's National Transit Database for the municipal systems that report service.
US Census Bureau. 2020 Census residents by block and the barrio outlines; American Community Survey 5-year estimates 2020-2024, table B25044. US Government works.
Data files. data/transit_reach.json (transit_reach.py) and data/barrios.geojson for the map.
