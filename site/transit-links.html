Connections between municipios

More than half of Puerto Rico's workers, 532,691 of 1,000,096 (53.3%), work in a municipio other than the one where they live (American Community Survey 2016-2020). Workers travel between 1,084 pairs of municipios, and a route with a published timetable joins 22 of those pairs. Those 22 pairs carry 26.8% of the workers who cross a municipal line. Another 34.5% travel between pairs that had a público route on the 2020 list, and 36.5% between pairs with no route listed. The other 2.2% travel between 6 pairs whose only timetabled route has a timetable that expired.

What this page is, and what it is not. It sets two open datasets side by side: where people travel to work, and where a transit route is listed. Where many people travel and no timetabled route is listed, it draws a simulated connection along the roads. A simulated connection marks a place to look at. It is not a proposed route, a service plan or a demand forecast, and it is not a position of Foundation for Puerto Rico on what service should exist. Públicos carry many of these trips without a timetable, so a pair with no timetabled route is not a pair with no service.

lightbulbFindings: where the gaps are

What the open data say about travel between municipios, from the workers who travel to the time a trip takes by timetable. Each finding is explained further down the page. They are facts to read side by side; Foundation for Puerto Rico publishes no ranking of where service should go, which is a decision for its leadership.

  1. Most travel to work crosses a municipal line, and little of it has a timetabled route. 532,691 workers (53.3%) work outside the municipio where they live. Only 26.8% of that travel is between pairs of municipios that a route with a current timetable joins; 34.5% has only a público route of the 2020 list and 36.5% has no route listed.
  2. By timetable, a trip between town centres is possible only in and around San Juan, and it is slow. Leaving between 6:00 and 9:00 on a weekday, 28 of the 1,084 pairs have a trip of three hours or less; the fastest takes a median of 4.5 times as long as driving.
  3. Most town centres have no timetabled stop nearby. 52 of the 78 have no stop of a weekday timetable within 1 km. Only 18 municipios publish a timetable for their own system; 6 have one that expired and 15 report service but publish none.
  4. 182 pairs have many workers and no timetabled route. Each has 500 workers or more travelling between them (280,269 in all); 81 have no route listed at all and 101 only a público route of 2020. The map draws each as a simulated connection along the roads, not as a proposed route.
  5. The roads those connections follow are busy, and many pass bridges in poor condition. Their counted roads carry a median of 32,349 vehicles a day, and 130 of the 182 pass a bridge in poor condition.
  6. One household in eight has no vehicle. 12.96% of Puerto Rico's households have no vehicle (American Community Survey 2020-2024); the tables give the share at both ends of every pair.
  7. Part of the gap is in the data. Públicos run without timetables and the only list of their routes is of 2020; many municipal systems publish no timetable; the open crash data hold only deaths. Where a pair shows no route, people may still travel by público or with a transfer.

map1 · Where timetables join municipios, and where they do not

Green lines join the 22 pairs of municipios that share a route with a current timetable; nearly all are in the San Juan area. Each follows the stretch of its busiest route between the two municipios, as the timetable draws it, the ferry crossings included. Dashed lines are the 182 simulated connections: pairs with 500 workers or more and no timetabled route. 81 of them have no route listed at all (orange) and 101 had a público route on the 2020 list (purple). The largest with no route listed are San Juan – Toa Baja (8,769); San Juan – Toa Alta (7,149); Cabo Rojo – Mayagüez (5,094). The largest with only a público route listed are Bayamón – Toa Alta (7,173); Juana Díaz – Ponce (6,585); Arecibo – Hatillo (5,842).

By the weekday timetables, a trip that leaves a town centre between 6:00 and 9:00 reaches the other town centre within three hours for 28 of the 1,084 pairs, all of them in and around San Juan. The fastest of those trips takes a median of 4.5 times as long as driving without traffic (Caguas to San Juan: 103 minutes by timetable, 26 by car). 52 of the 78 town centres have no stop of a weekday timetable within 1 km. The tables below give these figures for every pair and every municipio. They are facts to look at side by side, not a ranking of where service should go.

route4 · How to read a simulated connection

Timetabled route. A route of the Autoridad de Transporte Integrado or of a municipal system, with a timetable that still runs on 2026-10-29, has stops in both municipios; one stop just across the municipal line is enough, so look at the trips in the table. Where the only such route has a timetable that expired, the pair is marked so and gets no simulated connection. Only direct routes count. On the map, the green line is the stretch of the pair's route with the most weekday trips, from its point in each municipio nearest the town centre: a route that serves San Juan in Río Piedras is drawn to Río Piedras. Each stop counts in the municipio whose outline holds it. Where the table says a transfer joins a pair, the two municipios are linked through other municipios by timetabled routes, but no journey time was computed and the trip may be long.
Público route on the 2020 list. The Instituto de Estadísticas de Puerto Rico published 334 público routes of the Autoridad de Carreteras y Transportación on March 19, 2020; 147 of them join two municipios or more. A route joins the municipios where its line ends and those whose town centre it passes within 1 km. The list has no timetable and has not been updated, so a route on it may no longer run. The first step for any of these pairs is to confirm with the operators what runs today.
Time by timetable. For every pair, a program searched the published weekday timetables for the fastest trip between the two town centres, in either direction, leaving every 15 minutes from 6:00 to 9:00. It walks up to 1 km to the first stop and from the last, and up to 400 m between stops to transfer, with 2 minutes for each transfer. The table gives the fastest of those trips and how many times longer it is than driving without traffic; the usual trip, with the wait for the bus, is longer. 7 municipal systems publish times only for the ends of some routes, so trips that board or leave at their other stops are missed. Públicos are in no timetable.
Need at each end. The shares of households with no vehicle and of residents below the poverty level in each municipio come from the American Community Survey 2020-2024 (5-year), as on the Analysis tab. They describe the whole municipio, not the people who travel between the two.
Along a simulated connection. The residents of the 2020 Census blocks within 1 km of the connection's line and, in brackets, those of them more than 400 m in a straight line from a scheduled stop; and the bridges within 50 m of the line that the Federal Highway Administration rates in poor condition. Connections share roads, so these counts must not be added up.
No route listed. Neither source lists a route that joins the two municipios. People may still travel between them by car, by a público that is not on the 2020 list, or with a transfer.
The line. A simulated connection is the fastest path along the roads between the two town centres, for a pair with 500 workers or more. Together the 182 lines measure 4,548 km, counting shared roads once for each pair. The line shows a distance and a direction. Where a route would run, where it would stop and how often are questions for a transit study with the operators and the communities.
Traffic on its roads. The last column of the table gives, for each simulated connection, the traffic on the roads it follows: points every 100 m along the line take the annual average daily traffic of the counted main road within 25 m (FHWA's Highway Performance Monitoring System, data year 2025), and the column shows their median. Across the 182 connections the median is 32,349 vehicles a day. A busy road means people already drive that way; it does not say whether a bus would be used. The layer Traffic on the main roads shows every counted road.
Road deaths on its roads. The last column counts the people killed in fatal crashes within 100 m of each simulated connection over 2020-2024 (NHTSA's Fatality Analysis Reporting System), for every 10 km of the connection, so that long and short connections can be compared. Connections share roads, so the counts must not be added up. The observatory publishes only these five-year totals, never a crash's place or date. Across the 182 connections the middle value is 5.4 deaths per 10 km; the highest are Carolina – Guaynabo (18.5); Carolina – Cataño (18.3); Bayamón – Carolina (16.8).

infoMethod and limits

Workers. The Census Bureau's County-to-County Commuting Flows of the American Community Survey 2016-2020 count workers 16 and over by the municipio where they live and the municipio where they work. It is the newest release: the Bureau publishes these flows every five years, and the survey years include the start of the pandemic. For a pair, the page adds the two directions; the margin of error of the sum is the root of the sum of the squares of the two margins (90% confidence).
Only work trips. The flows count where people work. Trips to study, to a doctor, to shop or to visit family are not counted, and neither are people without a job, who often depend most on transit. The figures say how many people make the trip, not how they travel: most drive.
Timetables. A timetable shows the service an operator publishes, not the service that ran. Several municipal systems publish no timetable or let theirs expire, so some real routes are missing here. 7 municipal systems (Bayamón, Caguas, Jayuya, Juncos, San Lorenzo, San Sebastián and Vega Baja) time only the first and last stop of some routes; for those routes, the system's stops along the route's line count as its stops. The timetables are refreshed every night; this page's figures are rebuilt when the sources change.
Town centres and roads. Distances and times run between town centres along the roads mapped in OpenStreetMap, at typical speeds for each kind of road, without traffic. San Juan's centre is Old San Juan, though many of its jobs are in Hato Rey, Río Piedras and Santurce. Vieques and Culebra have no road to the main island, so their pairs have no distance and no simulated connection; the ferry from Ceiba is a timetabled route.
More detail. The method, the checks and the code are in docs/transit-links.md of the project repository; each figure has an entry in the data dictionary (search for 'between municipios').
