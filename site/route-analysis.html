Public transit route profile: crossings at high-volume intersections

This exploratory, comparative analysis examines, one by one, the bus routes that run in the San Juan metropolitan area and in the municipios of eastern Puerto Rico. Using the official weekday timetables (GTFS), each route is analyzed on five dimensions: the level of service, the route, the intersections with heavy traffic on its path at peak hours, the integration with other services and the population near its stops. The analysis focuses on crossings at high-volume intersections; the other four dimensions help compare the routes.

About the 69 of 74: 15 of them are estimates, marked with an asterisk in the table (note 3).

Foundation for Puerto Rico prepared this analysis for the PR Data Observatory with public data: the timetables of the ATI and of the municipal systems, the traffic of the Federal Highway Administration (HPMS 2024), OpenStreetMap and the 2020 Census.

Here an intersection with heavy traffic is one where an estimated 2,000 or more vehicles pass in an hour, in one of the busiest hours of the year (note 1). A bus can lose time there, but this analysis does not measure it: using each route's weekday timetable, the two charts count those intersections on its path. The Tren Urbano is left out: it does not share the street.

1. How the flow is estimated. It is the annual average daily traffic (AADT) of the busiest road within 30 m of the intersection, multiplied by its K factor: the share of AADT that passes in the design hour, the 30th-busiest hour of the year (FHWA, HPMS 2024, Field Manual item 26). So the 2,000 vehicles an hour apply to one of the busiest hours of the year, not to a typical weekday peak. HPMS requires K only on its Sample Panel sections; for 764 of the 1,582 intersections (48%) the road has none, and the observatory used the median K of the road class: its own estimate, which HPMS guidance does not endorse (it says statewide or system-wide values shall not be used).

compare_arrows2 · Route comparison

Each row is a route of the metro area or of the east (note 2). Sort by any column, see all the columns with the button, and select a row to see the card of the route.

Hovering over a heading shows its definition. * by the route: its crossings are an estimate from a timetable with times only at the ends of each trip (note 3). ! by the system: expired timetable.

Definitions. Trips: trips on a weekday, both directions. Frequency: every how many minutes it runs from 6 to 9 a.m., in the direction with most trips. High-volume intersections: those with heavy traffic (section 1) on its path. Crossings 6–9 a.m.: how many times its trips cross them in that period. Residents within 400 m: people (2020 Census) within 400 m of a stop, each counted once per route.

2. Regions. The metropolitan area includes the ATI (AMA) and the municipal systems with at least one stop within 250 m of an ATI stop: San Juan, Bayamón, Guaynabo, Caguas and Canóvanas. The east includes Fajardo, Humacao, Juncos, San Lorenzo, Patillas and Vieques, which run their own systems with no connection to the ATI network.

3. Timetable only. The timetables of Bayamón, Caguas, Juncos and San Lorenzo, and of ATI express route 20 (two stops), give the time only at the first and the last stop of each trip. For these routes the Km is the length of the route line the operator draws, which on some routes holds both directions (Bayamón BR-1: 32.1 km of line, 16.0 km between its two timed stops); the Km/h is that of the stretch between the two timed stops, and is left blank when that stretch is under half the line; and the crossings are estimated as the trips of the period times the high-volume intersections on the line.

4. The other columns (with the button and in the card of each route): Km and scheduled Km/h; intersections with heavy traffic Per 10 km; Crossings p.m. (4 to 7 p.m.); Train (a stop within 300 m of a station); Systems (other systems with stops within 250 m); and pedestrian Bridges within 30 m of its path.

5. Limitations. ATI route 20 (Estación Bayamón to Terminal Campanilla) is an express service with two stops, so few residents live near it. Some routes of San Juan and Canóvanas have scheduled speeds under 8 km/h, possibly because their timetable includes long waits. The routes of Humacao and Patillas are under 4 km long, with scheduled speeds under 6 km/h. Cataño and Toa Baja do not publish timetables in GTFS format, so they are not included.
