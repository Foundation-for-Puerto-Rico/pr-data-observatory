Budget and public spending

What the government's agencies were approved to spend, were proposed to spend and spent, by function and by agency. The approved budget for fiscal year 2027 (July 2026 to June 2027) is $33.58 billion. These are the agencies in the fiscal plan: public corporations with their own budgets, such as the electric and water utilities, and the municipal governments' own budgets are not included (ABRE Tu Municipio covers municipal finances), and no figure is placed in a municipio, because the budget names none. How public money moves: the stages behind these figures

Stage and basis. Proposed and approved are OGP's budgets; spent is what the agencies reported to OGP in the next budget process, not the audited close. Each fiscal year runs from July to June.

See the figures as a table

Each rectangle's area is its share of the budget chosen. The function of each budget line is assigned by a crosswalk reviewed on 9 November 2026. In the approved budget for fiscal year 2027, 5% of the money is “Other”, mostly the lottery, the Gaming Commission and lines of the custodial accounts that mix several purposes; for fiscal years 2023 and 2024, whose custodial lines use other codes, the share is larger.

Functions are approximate. About 75% of this budget takes the general function of its agency, not of its own program, because the data sheets do not say more. A function's total is a guide, not an exact figure; the Method section shows which agencies this affects most.
Names that are easy to confuse. The appropriations under the custody of Hacienda ($3.43 billion) are not the Treasury Department's own budget, which is a separate line ($1.04 billion): they are money it holds for the whole government, mostly transfers ($2.21 billion) and debt service ($1.04 billion). The appropriations under the custody of the Office of Management and Budget ($0.76 billion) are apart from that office's own budget in the same way. The Department of Housing ($3.29 billion, 99% of it federal funds) and the Public Housing Administration ($0.76 billion) are two bodies: the second is attached to the first and runs the public housing projects. Figures: approved budget, fiscal year 2027.

compare_arrows1 · Approved and spent

For fiscal year 2025 the data sheets give both the approved budget and what was spent: $29.23 billion spent of $33.28 billion approved. Spent is what the agencies reported during the next budget process; the official close is the Treasury Department's financial statements.

What the difference can mean. The $4.05 billion between approved and spent in fiscal year 2025 can come from reserves held back, payments scheduled after June 30, legal restrictions on some funds, accounting differences or later amendments to the budget; the two figures also come from two different OGP data sheets. The difference alone does not show inefficiency or a lack of capacity. The audited close is the Treasury Department's Basic Financial Statements, prepared on another accounting basis; the latest found as of November 2026 is for fiscal year 2023.

Stage and basis. Approved, from OGP's approved budget; spent, the “Gastado” column of OGP's data sheets, which is what each agency reported. Neither is audited, and this page reads no figure from the Treasury Department (Hacienda).

By agency and fund

Choose a fund to see, agency by agency, what was approved and what was reported as spent. The funds are never mixed, because each follows its own rules.

Each budget goes through stages: the Governor sends a proposed budget to the Oversight Board, the Board approves it or sends it back, the Legislature evaluates and adopts it, the Governor signs it, the Board certifies the budget that follows the fiscal plan, and the agencies spend it. The Board has no column of its own, but it is in two of the three: in OGP's own words, the proposed budget is the one the Governor presents to the Board and the Legislature, the approved budget is the one the Board certified, and the spent budget is the one used during the fiscal year. A missing stage is a dot.

payments2 · Where the money comes from

Every budget in the data sheets, by fund. The General Fund is mostly taxes. Federal funds are grants from the federal government that the agencies manage, such as the Nutrition Assistance Program and Medicaid; the same dollars also appear in USAspending as federal awards, so the two sources are never added together. Special funds are fees and other revenue set aside by law.

bar_chart3 · What the money pays for

The budget chosen above, by concept of expense. Transfers include the government health plan's payments, nutrition assistance and contributions to other entities; debt service and pensions are counted here whatever agency pays them.

Debt service is the payment of principal and interest on the government's debt: $1.08 billion in the approved budget for fiscal year 2027, 96% of it under the appropriations in Hacienda's custody. The Constitution of Puerto Rico (Article VI, section 8) orders that, when a year's resources do not cover its appropriations, interest and principal on the public debt are paid first.

location_city4 · Capital projects by municipio

Where the government plans to build: the Planning Board's Four-Year Investment Program for fiscal years 2026 to 2029 lists the capital projects of the agencies and public corporations, each with its municipio. It is a plan of recommended projects, not money approved or spent, and it includes public corporations, such as the Aqueduct and Sewer Authority, that the budget above leaves out. 86% of the money is in projects with a municipio; the rest is beside the map.

Stage and basis. Planned: projects the Planning Board recommends, not money approved, obligated or spent. Basis: the adopted program as published.

account_balance5 · Federal money in Puerto Rico

What federal agencies committed to spend on work or benefits in Puerto Rico, from USAspending.gov. In federal fiscal year 2025 (October 2024 to September 2025) the obligations add up to $38.44 billion; 73% are direct payments, mostly Social Security and Medicare benefits paid to people. The highest year was 2021, with $65.23 billion, when pandemic relief was added.

Stage and basis. Obligated: committed by federal agencies, not necessarily paid out. Basis: reported by the agencies to USAspending.gov, not audited. Each federal fiscal year runs from October to September.

A grant is not repaid; a loan is. The Loans figure is what the loans cost the federal government, not the amount lent.

home_work7 · Disaster recovery money by municipio

The Department of Housing manages the federal disaster recovery and mitigation grants of the Community Development Block Grant program (CDBG-DR and CDBG-MIT). USAspending places those grants where the Department is, in San Juan. The part the Department passes on as subawards, to municipalities, contractors and nonprofits, is reported where the work is done. This map shows those subawards.

Stage and basis. Subawarded: the part of the prime grants passed on to first-tier subrecipients, counted when it is made, not when it is paid out. Basis: reported by the Department of Housing under the federal transparency law, not audited.

The prime grants: obligated and paid out

Each CDBG-DR and CDBG-MIT grant to the Department of Housing: what HUD obligated over the grant's life and what the Treasury has paid out to the Department so far (its draws), from USAspending. HUD's own monthly reports for 1 September 2026 give draws within 2% of these for four of the six grants over $100 million, and lower for the mitigation grant (4%) and the 2020 earthquakes grant (19%); USAspending was read later, in November 2026. A draw pays the Department, not yet the work; a grant signed later has had less time to draw.

Read with care. Subawards are a part of the prime grants, not money on top of them, and they are already inside the federal money of section 5: never add the two. home_work8 · FEMA Public Assistance: stage and age

FEMA's Public Assistance pays to repair public roads, schools, utilities and other public facilities after a disaster. OpenFEMA lists 23,059 Puerto Rico projects with $39.05 billion of federal share obligated, and the step each project is in: closed out, open or pending a review. It gives obligations, not payments. The Disaster funding and need page has the same money by municipio with each disaster's damage and need.

Stage and basis. Obligated by FEMA, by each project's process step; reported in OpenFEMA, not audited. Closed out is FEMA's final reconciliation of a project's costs: an open project can be finished on the ground and still waiting for its documents.

location_city9 · My municipio

Choose a municipio to see, in one place, the public money in its territory that this page can place, who runs each part, the answers to four questions about its disaster recovery money, and what could be requested to evaluate how it is being spent.

A municipio's figures are the money that names it: much of the money is run island-wide and is in no municipio. The municipal government's own budget is not here; ABRE Tu Municipio covers its finances.

Why one municipio shows far more recovery money than another: besides the damage, FEMA Public Assistance is obligated project by project, for the projects an applicant requests and documents; and the large federal recovery grants to Commonwealth agencies are recorded in San Juan, where the agency is. Only what is passed on by subaward is placed where the work is reported.

infoMethod

The data sheets. The Office of Management and Budget (OGP) publishes one data sheet (sábana) for each approved and each proposed budget, with one line for each agency, fund, program, concept and object of expense. A newer sheet repeats earlier years; each fiscal year and stage on this page comes from the newest sheet whose lines add up to its own total, so every figure equals OGP's published total. A draft marked confidential that is posted on the budget site is not used.
Functions. Each agency has one function (Education, Health and so on); a few programs and the funds in custodial accounts have their own. Debt service and pensions are always Debt and pensions. The table of equivalences was reviewed on 9 November 2026 against the UN Classification of the Functions of Government (COFOG); the decisions are in the project repository.

Where each line's function comes from

A line takes its function from its own row of the table of equivalences when it has one: its program, its object of expense, or its concept when that is debt service or pensions. Otherwise it takes its agency's general function. An agency with mixed work, classified by its general function, hides the mix, so the largest such agencies come first in the next review.

Capital projects. Each project line of the program's agency tables is read with its municipio and its four fiscal years; a line counts only when its years add up to its total. A line that lists several municipios is split equally among them; a line for a region, the whole island or no defined place is shown beside the map. Per resident uses the Census Bureau's estimate for July 1, 2025. Project names are not shown.
Federal money. For each finished federal fiscal year, USAspending.gov's obligations with place of performance Puerto Rico are read once for each award type: assistance by program (assistance listing), contracts by awarding agency. Each program takes its function from the crosswalk, or else its agency's; a contract takes its agency's. A year counts as final from 15 November, about six weeks after it ends. Loans are the estimated cost to the government, not the amount lent.
Paid out. USAspending.gov's award search gives, for each grant and contract in Puerto Rico signed from federal fiscal year 2020, its obligation over its life and its outlays to date. An award counts up to its obligated amount; the note under the chart counts the exceptions. The two latest signing years are read again each year.
Sectors and data age. Section 6 reads the same files as the rest of the page. Per resident uses one population: the Census Bureau's estimate for July 1, 2025. Spending per public school student divides the Department of Education's spending by the students of the school year that matches the fiscal year (NCES Common Core of Data); the estimate by municipio splits that spending by each municipio's share of the students. Each data section says when its file was built and is marked when the file is older than two of its update cycles.
Disaster recovery. For each finished federal fiscal year, USAspending.gov's subawards with place of performance Puerto Rico are read for the Department of Housing's disaster grants only (grant numbers of the DR, MIT and electrical grid grants; the yearly block grants and the COVID grant are left out): once by municipio and once for Puerto Rico as a whole, so the subawards that name no municipio are shown apart. Per resident uses the same population as the capital projects map.
What the page does not do. It does not place the budget in municipios (the capital projects and disaster recovery maps place plans and subawards where the work is reported), compare Puerto Rico with other places, or say whether a function received enough. It shows where money is assigned, not what it achieved. Never add two budgets: they are different stages, or different years, of the same money.

Office of Management and Budget (OGP), Government of Puerto Rico. Budget data sheets (Sábana) of the approved and proposed budgets, www.presupuestos.pr.gov. Public documents of the Government of Puerto Rico; OGP states no license for them.
Law and roles. Who proposes, adopts and certifies the budget, what proposed, approved and spent mean, and the rule on money of earlier fiscal years: OGP's Manual of the Budget Process (gestion-presupuestaria.ogp.pr.gov, updated 18 November 2025) and PROMESA, section 202 (Public Law 114-187; 48 U.S.C. 2142). The order of payment when resources fall short: Constitution of Puerto Rico, Article VI, section 8. The Public Housing Administration: Act 66 of 1989, as OGP's budget document for the agency describes it (docs.pr.gov).
Junta de Planificación de Puerto Rico. Programa de Inversiones de Cuatro Años (PICA), adopted, jp.pr.gov. Public document of the Government of Puerto Rico; the Planning Board states no license for it. Population: U.S. Census Bureau, Population Estimates.
USAspending.gov, US Department of the Treasury. Federal awards by place of performance (spending_by_category API) and award outlays (spending_by_award API), www.usaspending.gov. No license is stated for the data; the API's source code is CC0-1.0.
Data and method. The data files are data/spending_state.json (ogp_budget.py), data/spending_pica.json (pica.py), data/spending_federal.json (federal_awards.py), data/spending_outlays.json (federal_outlays.py), data/spending_cdbgdr.json (cdbg_dr.py) and data/spending_fema_pa.json (fema_pa_stage.py); the method is in docs/spending.md of the project repository.
