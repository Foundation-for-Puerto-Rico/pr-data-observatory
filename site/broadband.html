Where broadband is needed in Puerto Rico

As of June 30, 2026, 54,180 of Puerto Rico's 1,177,315 broadband serviceable locations (4.6%) could not get home internet of 100/20 Mbps or more over a reliable technology, down from 104,909 in June 2023. These are the places where new or better broadband infrastructure may be needed, and the ones the federal BEAD program can fund. The municipios with the most such locations are Yabucoa (4,408), Cayey (2,788), Barranquitas (2,401).

Read with care. These figures are what internet providers report to the FCC they can install within ten business days: availability, not what households subscribe to or the speed they get. Providers' reports can be challenged and corrected, and the FCC's list of locations changes between releases.

The share of each municipio's locations below 100/20 Mbps in each June release from 2023 to 2026. Fiber reached 70.6% of Puerto Rico's locations in June 2026, from 46.3% in June 2023. Part of a change from one year to the next comes from the FCC's list of locations, not from the networks.

infoMethod

The locations. A broadband serviceable location is a place where the FCC says a home or business is, or could be, connected to fixed internet. An apartment building can be one location with many units, so a location is not a household.
Served, underserved and unserved. The BEAD program's definitions: a location is served when it can get 100/20 Mbps or more, underserved at 25/3 or more but less than 100/20, and unserved below 25/3. Only reliable technologies with low latency count: copper, cable, fiber to the premises, and licensed or licensed-by-rule fixed wireless. Satellite and unlicensed fixed wireless do not, so an unserved location may still have one of them.
Barrios and municipios. Each location belongs to its 2020 census block; each block to its municipio and, by the Census Bureau's internal point, to its barrio. The barrios add up exactly to the municipios and the municipios to Puerto Rico. The map's classes are the same in every year, so the years compare.
What the page does not say. It does not say what anyone pays, what speed they get or who provides the service, and it does not name any location or provider. It does not include mobile service. A location the FCC counts as served may still have no subscriber.
More detail. The full method, the checks and the code are in docs/broadband.md of the project repository; each figure has an entry in the data dictionary (search for 'broadband'). The same data are layers of the map, in the group Infrastructure · Broadband (FCC).
