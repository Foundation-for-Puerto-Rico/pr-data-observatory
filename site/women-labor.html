Women in the labor force by region

Select the region you want to analyze: The page opens with the 15 municipios of the Eastern Region (ERSV).

Each figure is an estimate from the Census Bureau's American Community Survey (ACS), with its margin of error at the 90% confidence level (±). The five-year window chosen above applies to every chart except section 5, which shows the three windows. Foundation for Puerto Rico prepared this analysis for the PR Data Observatory.

compare_arrows1 · The region, the rest of the island and Puerto Rico

Each dot is an estimate and each line its 90% margin of error. When the lines of two areas overlap a lot, the survey cannot tell their rates apart. The region is compared with the rest of Puerto Rico, not with the island total, because the island total includes the region itself.

groups2 · Who is behind the number

The rate divides women in the labor force by all women 16 and over. Women not in the labor force include students, retirees, women caring for family and women who would like to work but are not looking. Women looking for work count as in the labor force.

Sample size

The survey does not publish how many of the people interviewed were women 16 and over. The Census Bureau weights each answer to represent the whole population, and the margins of error already reflect the size of the sample. The number interviewed is published for 2020-2024 only.

elderly_woman3 · Age changes the picture

Participation rises from the late teens, peaks between 25 and 44 and falls after 55, and almost no woman 65 or older works. So an area's overall rate depends on how old its women are. The chart compares each age group; a diamond under an age group marks a difference that is statistically significant at 90%.

The age-adjusted rate answers: what would the area's rate be if its women had the age mix of the women in the rest of the island? The bar chart splits the difference between the two areas into a part due to the age mix and a part due to participation within each age.

family_restroom4 · Mothers and young children

For women 20 to 64 living in households, the survey also gives participation by the age of their own children. The survey does not give the ages of the women in each group, so part of a difference between groups may come from age. Since 2015-2019 the Census Bureau counts only the children of the householder, so this chart is not comparable with 2010-2014.

timeline5 · Over time

The ACS gives five-year estimates; only windows that share no year can be compared, so this chart uses 2010-2014, 2015-2019 and 2020-2024. Counts from different windows are controlled to different population estimates, so compare rates rather than numbers of people.

location_city6 · The municipios of the region

A regional rate can hide a wide spread between its municipios. Each row is a municipio of the selected region, with its 90% margin of error; small municipios have wide margins. The solid line is the region's pooled rate and the dashed line the rest of Puerto Rico.

lightbulb7 · Findings for the Eastern Region (ERSV), 2020-2024

In 2020-2024, 42.0% of women 16 and over in the 15 municipios of the Eastern Region were in the labor force, that is, working or looking for work. In the rest of Puerto Rico the rate was 41.9%. With the survey's margins of error, the two rates are statistically the same. The single number hides three things that matter for the Human Capital chapter: women still take part 9.0 points less than men; women 25 to 54 take part much more than the overall rate suggests, and more than a decade ago; and women 55 and over in the region leave the labor force earlier than in the rest of the island.

The region: Canóvanas, Ceiba, Culebra, Fajardo, Humacao, Juncos, Las Piedras, Loíza, Luquillo, Maunabo, Naguabo, Río Grande, San Lorenzo, Vieques and Yabucoa, as defined by FPR's Eastern Region Strategic Vision (ERSV). To see its charts, choose the button Eastern Region (ERSV, 15) at the top of the page.

A regional rate is not an average of municipio rates. The region's rate adds up its women in the labor force and divides by all its women 16 and over (42.0%). The simple average of the 15 municipio rates gives 42.6%, because it gives Culebra (about 606 women 16 and over) the same weight as Humacao (about 23,332). Use the pooled rate in the chapter.
  • Age: the region differs significantly from the rest of the island only at 55-64, 65+. 31.1% of women 55 to 64 in the region are in the labor force, against 37.4% in the rest of Puerto Rico (-6.4 points, ± 2.4). 27.4% of the region's women 16 and over are 65 or older.
  • Age-adjusted rate: 40.7%, against an actual 42.0%. The region's women are slightly younger than those in the rest of the island, which raises its rate by 1.3 points; lower participation after 55 lowers it by 1.2. Together they change the rate by +0.1 points, which is why the overall rates look the same.
  • Over time: from 2010-2014 to 2020-2024 the participation of women 16 and over rose 1.3 points (± 1.1) and that of women 25 to 54 rose 7.8 points (± 1.9).
  • Count: the estimated number of women 16 and over fell by about 9,000 from 2010-2014 to 2020-2024; counts from different windows are controlled to different population estimates, so part of that change comes from the controls.
  • Children: in the region and in the rest of the island, mothers of children under 6 (66.6% and 65.7%) take part more than women 20 to 64 without own children under 18 (55.7% and 56.6%); no difference between the region and the rest of the island is significant.
  • Municipios: from 64.0% in Culebra and 49.6% in Canóvanas to 32.5% in Humacao and 33.4% in Yabucoa; margins reach ± 10.4 points in Culebra and Vieques.
  • Sample: in 2020-2024 the survey interviewed 16,930 people of all ages in the region, about 4.1% of its residents (2020 Census).

infoMethod

Labor force participation rate. People 16 and over in the labor force (employed, or unemployed and looking for work; civilian or in the Armed Forces) divided by all people 16 and over, as in the Census Bureau's table S2301. The unemployment rate divides the unemployed by the civilian labor force. Table B23001 (sex by age by employment status).
Pooled rates for a group of municipios. The counts of the municipios are added, then divided. The margin of error of a sum is the square root of the sum of the squared margins, and that of a rate follows the Census Bureau's formula for a proportion (ACS General Handbook, chapter 8). These are approximations the Census Bureau recommends when the published tables do not cover the area.
Statistical significance. A difference is called significant when it is larger than its own margin of error at 90%, the square root of the sum of the two squared margins. An area is compared with the rest of Puerto Rico so that the two estimates do not include the same people. Windows that share years are never compared.
Age-adjusted rate and the split of the gap. Direct standardization: the area's rate in each of seven age groups, weighted by the age mix of the women 16 and over in the rest of the island. The difference with the rest of the island is split into a part due to age mix and a part due to participation within each age (Kitagawa's method, 1955). These two parts have no margin of error on the page.
Reliability. The coefficient of variation (CV) is the standard error divided by the estimate. Values with a CV over 30% are marked less reliable, the observatory's rule. Survey answers are weighted to represent the whole population.
