# Expert review: work items (2026-10-16)

Three views of the observatory, as a statistician, a digital-twin builder and a GIS analyst would read it, turned into work items. The views are Claude's reading of the code and documentation on 2026-10-16 (main at `89d4223`), **not a review by outside experts**; item X1 asks for one.

Each item says what already exists (checked in the code), what is missing, and when it counts as done. Status: **open**, **in progress** (a branch or pull request exists), **done**, or **decision** (waits on the project lead, leadership or Compliance). Nothing here changes data or code; each item gets its own branch and pull request, one at a time, in the usual flow.

## Order

1. G1 walking distance (in progress: tourism places in PR #41, residents next).
2. S1 and S2, uncertainty and overlapping survey years on every map and chart.
3. T2, one simulated scenario (sea level rise).
4. X1, an outside methods review before the site is promoted.

The rest follow in the order of the table, or sooner when a related change touches the same code.

## Statistician

| Id | Push-on | What exists today | Work | Done when | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Show uncertainty on the maps, not only in tables | Margins of error are published for the small-area survey data (`acs_tract.json`, `acs_blockgroup.json`, `acs_barrio.json`). `core/reliability.js` flags a value whose margin is above 30% of the estimate. The Economy tab hatches those places on its map, and the Housing page uses the same rule. The 2D map's municipality shading (`app.js`) and the 3D columns do not mark them. | Use the same hatch (a pattern, not a color) wherever an ACS value with a margin is shaded or raised: 2D shading, 3D columns, area profiles. Say in the legend what the hatch means. | Every map or column view of a survey estimate marks the unreliable ones, and a test pins the rule to `reliability.js`. | open |
| S2 | Do not compare overlapping five-year survey windows | The Housing page says that neighboring ACS windows share four of five years and points to non-overlapping ones (2012, 2017, 2022). The same sentence is in the Source column of the engine's per-area CSV download (`core/adapters.js`), not on screen. It is not said where a change is computed or drawn: change against a base year on the Economy tab and the workbench, trend lines, area profiles. | List every view that computes or charts a change between ACS windows, and put the note there, at the point of comparison. Where the page picks the base year, default to a window that does not overlap. | No view computes or charts a change between overlapping windows without the note beside it. | open |
| S3 | Make the class breaks visible, and the method a choice | The Economy tab's layer card (`core/panel.js`) already offers quantiles, equal intervals, natural breaks and own limits; the link keeps the choice and the legend shows the break values. The 2D map's municipality shading (`app.js`) and the Housing page (`housing.js`) are fixed to five quantile classes and show no method choice. | Give the 2D municipality shading and the Housing maps the same class control and legend as the Economy tab, reusing `core/panel.js` and `core/classify.js`. | Every shaded map shows its breaks and method and lets the reader switch, and the link keeps the choice. | open |
| S4 | Say when a rate mixes sources or years | Dictionary entries give each field's source and year, one field at a time. | List every published rate whose numerator and denominator come from different sources or years (for example a survey estimate over the 2020 census population). Name both in its entry, and add a contract check that each rate names its denominator. | Every such rate says so in the dictionary and on hover, and the contract fails when a new one does not. | open |
| S5 | Warn once about reading areas as people | No page or entry explains it (the ecological fallacy). | One plain sentence on the area profiles and the municipality panel, and a methods entry in the dictionary: an area's figures describe the area, not each person in it. | The note is on both views in English and Spanish. | open |
| S6 | Counts of places are listings, and the 0.5 cut is a choice | The dictionary says counts are Overture listings, not businesses or visitors. The map's confidence slider lets a reader change the cut. No document shows how much the headline shares move with it. | Recompute the island and municipal headline shares at confidence 0.3, 0.5 and 0.7, and publish the table in the methods docs. | The table exists and the dictionary links to it. | open |
