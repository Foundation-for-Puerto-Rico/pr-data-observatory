# The layer menu: groups, order and the 2D and 3D views

The "Layers" tab of the 2D map (`site/index.html`) and of the 3D view (`site/3d.html`) share one menu: the same groups, in the same order, with the same names, in both languages. The list is `site/js/core/layers.js`. The project lead asked for it on 2026-10-19 (HANDOFF decision 55): the same organization in 2D and 3D, and every layer in both views where it can be drawn. Since navigation PR 5 the groups are the topics of the Explore tab (decision 81 (e)).

## The groups

**By topic since navigation PR 5 (2026-11-05, HANDOFF decision 81 (e)).** The groups are the topics of the Explore tab: Reference first, then each domain of `dictionary_src/domains.json` that has a layer, in that order and with the domain's name, and the visitor economy last. A layer sits in the group of its home domain (its `domain` in `layers.js`); a sub-domain is a subheading. The domains with no point or line layer (Population and households, Economy, Public money, Health) have no group: their data are per-area datasets, put on the map from Explore, the Catalog or Rankings. Before PR 5 the menu ran from the macro to the micro in seven groups (decision 55: Territory; Hazards and environment; Land and housing; Infrastructure and mobility; Community services; Communities; Visitor economy).

Each row names its own source in a small note; group titles name only the topic. Within a group: areas, then lines, then points, with two exceptions:

- **Referencia:** the boundary lines (Límites municipales, Barrios) come before the 3D columns. They are the reference the shading and the columns are read against, and Límites is on when the page opens, so it heads the menu (after the 3D scene in 3D).
- **Infraestructura y movilidad:** the 400 m walk rings come after the stop, station and taxi points. They ring those points, and they are drawn only while stops or stations are on, so the switch follows the layers it depends on.

Every layer that can be drawn in both views is in both, with the same id, name, source, pop-up and privacy rules. The rows in one view (the 3D scene's terrain and buildings, the 3D columns, the vehicle replay, and slope, contour lines and watersheds, added on the 2D map first) are listed at the end, each with its reason.

### Dataset groups (Analysis tab, Rankings, 3D columns)

`sortGroups` in `site/js/core/datasets.js` orders the groups by the same topics, in the order of the domains: population (People, Population · …); the economy (Jobs and employers, Labor force, Industries, Income and poverty, Workers and commuting, Economy · …); public money; housing (Housing · …); health (Health care · …, Health · …); education (Education · …); environment and climate (surface and night heat, vegetation, water and air, regulated sites, wetlands, Land use (PUT 2015), residents and housing units in protected areas); hazards (residents and housing units in flood zones and below sea level rise, then the other Environment · … groups: natural hazard risk, landslides, earthquakes, community resilience); infrastructure and mobility (Transit access, residents and housing units near stops, airports and ferries, street connectivity, climb, Public safety, emergency services, Infrastructure · …); communities (Vulnerability (WCRP) · …); a group that matches none; Tourism supply last. A group goes where the longest beginning it starts with stands, so a new "Environment · …" group lands in Hazards unless its beginning is listed under Environment and climate. The group names themselves are unchanged.

## How the menu is built and checked

Both menus stay **static markup** in their pages. The registry does not draw them; `tests/layers_menu.test.js` reads both pages and fails when they differ from it. Why static:

- **Ids.** About 270 dictionary view links, `map_layers.json`, the catalog's actions, the Analysis tab's context switches, the chat (`CH_CONTEXT`, `ask3d.js` `CTX`), `network.js`, `core/facilities.js`, legends, counts and help keys name the elements by id. In the markup they stay where they were; nothing waits for a renderer.
- **Tests.** `test_budget.py` reads `<input type="checkbox" id="lyPlaces">` in `3d.html` (the places start off); `test_catalog.py` reads `app.js` `SHADE_DS` (the retired `#choro` measures, navigation PR 4b); the chip's tests read the pages' mount calls. All keep working unchanged.
- **Language.** Static text is translated by `I18N.apply()` from each page's own scope (`map`, `threed`), so no shared scope or new bundle is needed. The test checks that the Spanish of every title, subheading, name and translated source in each page's bundle is the registry's, and `i18n.py --check` already fails when the same English has different Spanish in two scopes.
- **No startup work.** A rendered menu would have to set checkboxes and fire no change events at startup (the 3D view starts with terrain, buildings, routes and trips on; the 3D places stay off). Static markup does nothing.
- **Weight.** The pages do not load `layers.js`; the map's scripts grow only by the chip's ordering and the group order.

What the test checks, for each view: the groups, their order, titles and icons (and that each icon is in the page's icon font); every data group is in both views; in each group, the rows and subheadings of that view in order; every switch of the Layers tab is in the registry: any checkbox, wherever it stands (in a `.layer` row, in a `label.toggle` such as the walk rings, or before its label) and any `select[data-layer-select]`, except the controls of a row, which the test lists with their row (`NOT_LAYERS`: the places-of-worship filter; checkboxes with no id are the land-use classes, `data-lu`); a row's name, its source (`<small class="src">`, with `translate="no"` when the source is a name) and whether it is counted (`class="layer nocount"`); a group with no row in a view carries a note (`data-elsewhere`; since phase B no group needs one); the `l=` keys of `app.js` `LAYER_KEYS` are the published ones; the "tourism last" rules above; the topic order (the domains of `domains.json` that have layers, in its order, each group named as its domain, every row in the group of its home domain); that "Also show on the map" holds its whole list (Límites and the seven layers the Analysis tab sets from the chat, `ecSetContext`), in the menu's order and with the menu's names; that `sortGroups` lists each group once; that `oneView()` is the four rows below, each with its reason; and that the chat's context words (`chat.js` `CH_CONTEXT`) name only layers both views draw, which both switch on (`ask3d.js` `CTX`, `economy.js` `ecSetContext`).

## Adding a layer

1. **Registry.** Add the row to its group in `site/js/core/layers.js`, in place (areas, then lines, then points): `{ id, key, en, es, src, views }`. `src` is a string when it is a name the same in both languages (FEMA, NOAA, OpenStreetMap), or `{ en, es }`. `views` is `['2d']`, `['3d']` or both (`B`). `counted: false` for a reference layer that the group count and the chip leave out.
2. **Markup.** Write the row in each page of its views, where the registry puts it:
   `<div class="layer"><label><input type="checkbox" id="lyX"><span class="sw" style="background:#…"></span><span class="ly-name">Name <small class="src" translate="no">SOURCE</small></span></label><button type="button" class="help-btn" data-help="x" aria-label="What is this?">…</button></div>`
   Its legend, count slot (`<small id="…">` after the source) or controls go right after it, in the same group.
3. **Spanish.** Add the name (and a translated source) to the page's scope: `i18n_src/es/map.json` for the 2D map, `threed.json` for the 3D view, with the registry's Spanish. Run `python i18n.py`.
4. **Wiring.** 2D: a key in `LAYER_KEYS` (`app.js`; it is the `l=` key in links, never changed once published), its loader and `onchange`, and its line in `dictionary_src/map_layers.json` if the Catalog should switch it on. 3D: its `onchange` in `twin3d.js`, its id in the chip's switch list (`Core.activelayers.switches([...])`) and, when it loads a file, the file under the 3D route's `on_use` in `dictionary_src/routes.json`. The drawing order is separate from the menu order: Leaflet panes in 2D; in 3D an area goes in with `areaBefore(id)` (under the barrio and municipal lines, in the order of `AREAS`), a point or circle layer with `slotBefore(prefix)` (above the areas and the columns, under the base map's labels, in the order of `SLOTS`; the route lines are its bottom slot), and the places with `PLACES_UNDER`. In 3D a layer that a click answers says what it is once, with `clickable(id, kind, card)` (see "Clicks" below); a deck.gl layer with a card goes in `Supply.card` (`supply3d.js`) or `deckCard` (`twin3d.js`).
5. **Check.** `node --test tests/layers_menu.test.js`, `python i18n.py --check`, and bump the `?v=` of what changed.

A new group is one more entry in `GROUPS` and an `h2.sec` heading in both pages (Economy, icon `work`, was added this way for the Opportunity Zones 2.0 layer, decision 103; its icon is in both pages' font lists). Keep tourism last. A subheading is a row `{ head: { en, es } }`, written `<h3 class="sec lsub">…</h3>`; it shows in a view only over rows of that view.

## Phase B: the 2D layers in the 3D view (2026-10-19)

The 3D view (`twin3d.js`, `facilities3d.js`) now draws every 2D layer that can be drawn there, from the same files, with the same switch ids, names, sources and pop-ups:

- **Límites municipales** (`lyMuni`): the switch of the municipal lines the view always drew (`muni-line`). On when the view opens and never counted, as on the 2D map; a click on a municipality still opens its summary when the lines are off.
- **Barrios** (`lyBarrios`): `barrios.geojson`, dashed lines under the municipal lines and an invisible fill for clicks. A click inside one adds the 2D map's section to the area card (`barrioPopup`, now in `access.js`, which both pages load; see "Clicks").
- **Paradas** (`lyStops`): the 4,240 stops of `transport.json` (the file the view already loads at the start), clustered with their count up to zoom 14 and single points from zoom 15, as the 2D clusters (`clusterRadius` 40). A click on a cluster zooms in and opens no card (it takes the click, so the route, municipality or barrio under it does not answer); a click on a stop shows the 2D card (`transportPopup`, in `access.js`). Stations, terminals and airports use the same card now (they had a shorter one with no source line).
- **Taxis y carga de vehículos eléctricos** (`lyTaxi`): the taxi stands, EV chargers, bike rentals and helipads of `transport.json`, colored by kind, with the same card.
- **The 400 m walk rings** (`walkRings`): a circle around every stop, bus station, Tren Urbano station and ferry terminal, drawn from zoom 14 while stops or stations are on, as on the 2D map. The radius is in meters on the ground: a MapLibre circle whose radius doubles at each zoom (`['interpolate', ['exponential', 2], ['zoom'], …]`) from its value at zoom 0, 400 × 512 / (40,075,016.686 × cos latitude) pixels, flat on the map (`circle-pitch-alignment: map`). Checked in the browser: the radius equals the projected 400 m to 0.01 px at pitch 0. The chip counts the rings while they are drawn.
- **Servicios comunitarios and Residenciales públicos** (`lyFire`, `lyPolice`, `lyTownhalls`, `lyLibraries`, `lyHistoric`, `lyPubHousing`): `js/facilities3d.js`, a module that loads on first use with `js/core/facilities.js` and the Spanish bundle `es.threed.facilities.js` (`I18N.load('facilities')`). `core/facilities.js` holds what both views share: the layers (file, color, icon, label, source, credit), the pop-up (name, type, municipio, listing date, source; no contact field), the note under each switch and the help texts. The files hold only what may be drawn, so the 3D view applies the same rules without code of its own: HUD developments that HUD placed only at the center of a block group or tract, NRHP private homes and haciendas and libraries whose point is in another town are counted (a dataset in Explore) and not drawn, and the note says so ("172 of 304 drawn…", "277 points for 269 of 342 listings; 73 private homes and haciendas are counted by municipio (a dataset in Explore) but not drawn.", "1,101 of 1,229 drawn…"). In 3D the note names where that view shows the counts: "(Columns by municipality → Any dataset)" / "(Columnas por municipio → Cualquier conjunto de datos)". The 2D map's `facilities.js` keeps only its Leaflet drawing and loads the core file first (`app.js` `facLoad`).
- **Residentes lejos del servicio and Puntos de integración** (`lyGaps`, `lyXfer`): in the 3D Route tab, under "Planning layers", as in the 2D Transit tab, with the same notes and texts (`population_blocks.json`, loaded on use, and `operators.json`, already loaded). A click shows what the 2D tooltip shows.
- **The 2D shading's measures as columns:** the 13 measures of `#choro` the columns lacked (median gross rent, vacant units, rent change, Suelo Urbano, residents within a 400 m walk of any scheduled stop, transit stops and stations, Uber's listing, places in a flood hazard area, inside protected areas, with a website, within an 800 m walk, with Saturday and with Sunday service). The Uber listing is a yes/no: listed municipalities stand at one height, the others are a flat gray sheet. Rent change can fall, so it uses the diverging scale (red loss, blue gain). The three "shown" measures are left out (see below).

**Drawing order.** The areas (flood, land use, protected areas, Comunidades Especiales, sea level) go in under the barrio and municipal lines; the four that a click answers are stacked in a fixed order whichever was switched on first (`AREAS`, `areaBefore()`: land use, protected areas, flood zones, Comunidades Especiales from the bottom, as the panes of the 2D map). The route lines and the new points and circles go above the areas and the columns and under the base map's labels (`slotBefore()`, from the bottom: route lines, gaps, walk rings, stops, taxis, integration points, facilities), so no point hides a flood or land-use area, and every point is drawn over the route lines, as on the 2D map, whose routes pane is under its markers (since 2026-10-19; the route lines were on top before, and a click on a stop on a line needed a rule of its own). Stations, WCRP pins and the planned route stay on top, in that order from the bottom whichever was switched on first (`TOPS`, `topBefore()`). The places' points are over the route lines, as on the 2D map; their heat map stays under the route lines (`heatBefore`), which would otherwise vanish under it in San Juan. What a click opens is in "Clicks", below.

**Loading.** Nothing new loads at startup: every new switch starts off (Límites starts on, but its lines were already drawn), and each file loads the first time its layer is switched on. The 3D route of `dictionary_src/routes.json` lists the view's layer files under `on_use`, the old ones and the new (`tests/test_budget.py` checks the new ones are not fetched at the start). The view still starts with terrain, buildings, routes and vehicles on and the places off.

**Chip, chat.** Every new switch is in the 3D chip (`Core.activelayers.switches`), so "N layers on", "Turn all off" and Undo cover them; Límites stays out, as on the 2D map. The chat's context words (`chat.js` `CH_CONTEXT`) now name the stops, stations, taxis, public housing and the five service layers ("muestra las estaciones de bomberos" switches on the fire and EMS stations beside the dataset's columns), and both views switch them on: the 3D view through `ask3d.js` `CTX`, the 2D map through `ecSetContext`, which switches these point layers on and never off; since the review of PR #75 the stops follow the same rule (a later answer no longer turns them off). The six area and line layers it had (barrios, flood zones, protected areas, land use, routes, tourism places) keep their rule: exactly what the question asks.

## Clicks

One click opens what is drawn on top, whichever layer was switched on first, and the 3D view follows the 2D map's rules (since 2026-10-19; before, the order in which the layers were switched on could decide). `twin3d.js` has one click handler. Each MapLibre layer that a click answers says once what it is, with `clickable(id, kind, card)`: `cluster`, `point`, `line`, `area`, or `base` (the municipalities). In order:

1. **Route planner.** While the planner is choosing a point ("choose on the map", or "Add points by clicking the map"), the click places the point and does nothing else: no card, and no zoom into a cluster of stops.
2. **deck.gl first.** deck.gl draws above every MapLibre layer, so an object of a deck.gl layer with a card answers first: the stops and routes of "Service at this hour", the stops of a route profile and of its connections, a storm track. A stop of the hour over the same stop of the Stops layer shows the hour's card, a route of the hour over a route line shows the hour's card, and a stop of the hour over a cluster shows its card without zooming. A storm track has no card of its own: a click shows in a card what pointing at it shows (on a phone there is no pointing).
3. **The feature on top.** Otherwise `map.queryRenderedFeatures(point)` lists the features at the pixel top first, in drawing order, and the first one decides: a cluster of stops (or its count) zooms in and opens no card; a point or a line opens its card. The points are drawn over the route lines (above), so a stop or a cluster on a line answers, not the line.
4. **Areas: one card.** When the feature on top is an area, one card lists every area under the click, as on the 2D map. Each area has a section built by the same function as on the 2D map (`access.js`: `floodPopup`, `protPopup`, `landusePopup`, `comunidadPopup`, `barrioPopup`, joined by `areasPopup`), with the same limits: two flood zones, two protected areas, one land-use class, one Comunidad Especial and one barrio. The visible areas come in their drawing order, top first. The barrio comes last, as on the 2D map: its fill is invisible. Last is the link "Open the <name> profile", which opens the municipality's card: the municipality of the ground under the click (`muni-hit`), since with the columns on and the camera tilted a column of another municipality can be drawn over the pixel. Since the areas are stacked in a fixed order (above), the card at a place is the same whatever order the layers were switched on in. The flood and protected-area sections are the 2D map's now: the 3D card used to give the FEMA subtype and the PAD-US designation code as stored; it now spells them out and adds public access and the GAP status.
5. **Layers with no card.** The vehicles of the replay and the line of a route profile have no card. A click on them opens no area or municipality card, but a point, line or cluster drawn under them still answers: the vehicles move, and a stop under a passing trail would not answer otherwise. These layers are pickable only for this one query (`noCardAt`), because deck.gl picks every pickable layer on each move of the pointer and the vehicles take about 7 ms a pick (measured). deck.gl applies the change in its next frame, so while vehicles or a profile are drawn, an area's card opens two frames after the click.
6. **Nothing.** With nothing under the click, land opens the municipality's card and the sea opens nothing.

A card opens with its top in view and the focus on its close button (`focusAfterOpen: false`): MapLibre would focus the first link, which in a card of several areas is at the bottom, and scroll the card down to it. On a narrow map (under 700 px, a phone) a card opens above or below the point, and its box moves in to stay 8 px inside the map while its tip stays on the point (`fitPop`, also while the map moves).

The planned route and the walk rings have no card and take no click, as on the 2D map: the click answers for what is under them. Hover tooltips are as before. The query asks only for the layers that are shown: with the terrain on (the default), each `queryRenderedFeatures` reads the terrain's coordinates back from the GPU for each source it looks in, and a hidden layer cost as much as a shown one while giving nothing (a click: about 40 ms with 14 layers, 15 to 21 ms with the 8 shown, measured). The pointer cursor over a clickable layer (`hoverCursor`) is one query of the shown layers at most once a frame, and none during a drag; MapLibre's own `mouseenter` and `mouseleave` ran one query per layer and per listener on every move of the pointer: with flood zones, barrios and stops on, 8 queries and 20 to 50 ms a move before, 1 query and about 5 ms after (measured). `tests/browser/click_order.js` checks each rule with real mouse clicks, in two pages that switch the layers on in opposite orders, in Spanish and English, at 1,400 and 390 px wide. `tests/browser/cluster_clicks.js` checks the clusters in both views.

## Layers in one view only

`Core.layers.oneView()` lists them, each with its reason (`why`), and `tests/layers_menu.test.js` requires exactly these:

- **3D terrain** and **3D buildings** (Escena 3D): settings of the 3D scene. The 2D map is flat.
- **Columns by municipality** (3D): a column is a height, which a flat map cannot show; the 2D map shades the municipios instead.
- **Scheduled vehicles** (3D, GTFS timetables replayed): the replay runs on the 3D time bar (deck.gl `TripsLayer`). The 2D map shows the vehicles on their timetable now instead (Transit tab, `lyNow`).
- **Watersheds and streams** (2D, `watersheds.geojson` and `streams.geojson`, USGS WBD and NHDPlus HR; 2026-11, GIS tools step 3, `docs/watersheds.md`): on the 2D map first; its code is in `js/terrain.js`, loaded on first use.
- **Slope** (2D, `slope_surface.png`, USGS 3DEP 10 m) and **Contour lines** (2D, drawn in the browser from the terrain tiles of the 3D view): added on the 2D map first (2026-11, GIS tools step 2; `docs/slope.md`). The 3D view shows the terrain itself. Their script (`js/terrain.js`) and its Spanish load the first time either is switched on.
- **Shade municipalities by** (2D): the 3D view shows the same measures as columns (height and color by value), so a shading draped under the columns would repeat them. Its three "shown" measures (tourism places shown, per 1,000 residents, per km²) count the places the 2D filters let through (minimum confidence, places of worship), which the 3D view does not have.

The 2D map's land-use class filter and analysis, its places' confidence slider, worship switch and hexagons, and the 3D view's places drawing (heat or points) are controls of a layer in one view, not layers: both views have the layer.
