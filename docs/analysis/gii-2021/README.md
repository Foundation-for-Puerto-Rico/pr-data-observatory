# Global Innovation Index: FPR's 2021 profile of Puerto Rico

The Knowledge Area tab "Índice Global de Innovación / Global Innovation Index" (`site/gii.html`, `site/js/gii.js`, `site/css/gii.css`) shows the Global Innovation Index profile of Puerto Rico that FPR's Centro de Innovación calculated in 2021, kept as printed, with the newest open value of each indicator beside it. Requested by Francis on 2026-10-23; his decisions are HANDOFF decision 59.

**FPR's calculation, not an official WIPO ranking.** Puerto Rico is not in the official Global Innovation Index. The method is that of the GII 2020 (13th edition; Cornell University, INSEAD and WIPO). The profile has no overall score, sub-index or pillar score, and none is computed here.

## Keyless updates (2026-10-29, branch `gii-keyless`)

Three indicators that the first version left as "needs a key" were checked again against keyless downloads. Each file was downloaded on 2026-10-29 and its Puerto Rico values checked against the 2021 value and FPR's method. `gii.py` keeps only the few numbers it needs in `cache/gii/` (`eia_INTL_PRI.json`, `census_pep_PR.json`, `bls_oews_PR.json`), never the downloaded file.

**3.2.1 Electricity output, updated (FPR estimate).** FPR's method: generation in GWh divided by the population in millions (2017: 16,360 GWh of PREPA / 3.325 million). Keyless sources: the EIA's bulk file of international energy data, `https://api.eia.gov/bulk/INTL.zip` (24,264,743 bytes; one JSON series per line; Puerto Rico is an entity, `PRI`), series `INTL.2-12-PRI-BKWH.A`, net electricity generation in billion kWh; and the Census Bureau's state totals, which include Puerto Rico (`NST-EST2025-ALLDATA.csv`, 53,555 bytes; `gii.py` looks for the newest vintage). Check against the original: for 2017 the EIA gives 16,370.7 GWh (PREPA's 16,360 differs by 0.07%) and the Census Bureau's Vintage 2020 3,325,284 people, so the method gives 4,923.1 against the printed 4,920.0. Result: 19,999.9 GWh / 3,202,521 people = **6,245.0 GWh per million (2024)**, against 4,920.0 in 2017, the year of Hurricane María (the EIA has 20,642.7 GWh for 2016). The arrow is "better" (+1,325.0; the threshold is 243.3, 5% of the GII 2026 interquartile range). No GII 2026 position: the GII takes gross production from the International Energy Agency ("measured at the terminals of all alternator sets", Index Structure sheet), and the EIA's figure is net of what the plants use.

**5.1.1 Knowledge-intensive employment, updated (FPR estimate).** FPR's method: the employment of the occupations (SOC codes) that match ISCO-08 groups 1 to 3, divided by total employment, from the BLS OES for Puerto Rico. Keyless source: the OEWS state file, `https://www.bls.gov/oes/special-requests/oesm25st.zip` (7,563,829 bytes, May 2025 estimates; one .xlsx, read with the standard library). bls.gov answered the download with `gii.py`'s descriptive User-Agent (`pr-digital-twin/1.0 (Foundation for Puerto Rico; open-data build)`); its HTML pages and HEAD requests answer 403, and a file that does not exist yet also answers 403, so `gii.py` tries May of last year, then the year before. When `FRESHNESS_CONTACT` is set, `gii.py` adds it to the User-Agent for bls.gov, as `freshness.py` does. Mapping: FPR's 2021 list of detailed SOC codes is in its working file, not in the repository, so the SOC 2018 **major groups** are used: 11 management, 13 business and financial operations, 15 computer and mathematical, 17 architecture and engineering, 19 life, physical and social science, 21 community and social service, 23 legal, 25 educational instruction and library, 27 arts, design, entertainment, sports and media, 29 healthcare practitioners and technical (the BLS's "management, professional and related occupations"), taken as ISCO groups 1 to 3; 31 healthcare support and 33-53 (service, sales, office, construction, maintenance, production, transport) are left out. The major-group totals are used rather than the sum of the detailed occupations because they include the detailed estimates that BLS suppresses ("**", 30 of 563 in Puerto Rico in May 2025). Result: 274,000 / 938,710 = **29.19% (May 2025)**. The same grouping gives 29.57% for May 2019 and 30.18% for May 2020, not the printed 32.0, so FPR's list included occupations outside these groups or left some out; the value is therefore not compared with 32.0 ("—", `chg_why` "method"). No GII 2026 position: the GII takes the ILO's labour force survey share, which counts every employed person; OEWS counts wage and salary jobs in establishments and leaves out the self-employed. To compare with 2021, someone with FPR's working file could copy its SOC list (codes only) into `gii.py`.

**IN.3.3.2 Low-carbon energy use (new in the GII 2026), FPR estimate.** Not one of the 80 indicators, so it appears in "Changes in the index since 2020" (`gii_changes.json`, `new_2026`: `pr` "fpr" and `pr_est`), not in the profile. From the same INTL.zip: consumption from nuclear, renewables and other (`INTL.4419-2-PRI-QBTU.A`) as a share of total energy consumption (`INTL.44-2-PRI-QBTU.A`): 0.004176 / 0.304692 quadrillion Btu = **1.37% (2024)**. The GII's definition (Index Structure sheet) is the share of total energy supply, with the physical energy content method: wind and solar electricity at 100% efficiency, 33% for nuclear and solar thermal, 10% for geothermal. The EIA counts wind, solar and hydro electricity at 3,412 Btu per kWh, the same physical energy content, and Puerto Rico has no nuclear or geothermal power, so the main difference is the source (EIA consumption, not the Energy Institute's supply). Labelled an FPR estimate; no position is given.

**6.1.1 Patent applications by origin, not updated.** FPR's method: 108 USPTO applications (2018) / 126.158 billion PPP$. No keyless source was found on 2026-10-29: WIPO counts Puerto Rico within the United States; PatentsView's download pages now open the USPTO Open Data Portal (`data.uspto.gov`, behind a bot check), its old file addresses on `s3.amazonaws.com/data.patentsview.org` answer 403, and the Portal's API answers 401 without a key; the USPTO's Patent Technology Monitoring Team reports under `uspto.gov/web/offices/ac/ido/oeip/taf/`, which counted granted patents by state of the first-named inventor (grants, not applications: a different measure), answer 404. The page's reason was rewritten (`NOT_UPDATED` in `gii.py`; the frozen original keeps its 2026-10-23 text).

### Why a raw-value position equals the score position

The GII ranks each indicator by its 0-100 score (WIPO's own rule, checked in the 2026 database: rank = 1 + the economies with a strictly higher score, for 78 of the 79 indicators; in 1.1.1 six near-tied economies are two places off). The score is a monotone function of the value: min-max (exactly 100 × (x − min)/(max − min) for 47 indicators, inverted for the five where higher is worse) or, for 9 indicators, min-max of ln[(max − 1)(x − min)/(max − min) + 1] (the GII 2020 Appendix IV formula, which reproduces those 9 scores exactly, the number WIPO's Appendix I gives). So placing Puerto Rico's raw value among the raw values gives the same place as placing its score, except where winsorization caps the extremes; `gii.py` gives no position when Puerto Rico's value lies beyond the values that are not capped (`free_lo`, `free_hi` in the extract). Ties share the better place. Adding Puerto Rico does not change the min and max of any compared indicator (its value lies inside the range), so no other economy's score would move. N = the economies with data + 1. The reference year is the year most economies use for that indicator (`c26_year`); for the WGI and the IMF Puerto Rico's value for that year is used, otherwise its latest.

No pillar, sub-index or overall score is computed: with comparable sources Puerto Rico has far fewer than the 36 input and 16 output indicators the GII requires (66% coverage), so the method cannot be replicated faithfully.

What is published from the database: for each compared indicator, its code, the year most economies use, the number of economies with data and the 25th, 50th and 75th percentiles (Python `statistics.quantiles`, inclusive). No economy is named. These cut-points are inclusive quartiles, not interpolated values chosen to avoid the data: a cut-point can coincide with one economy's value (the median in 8 of the 10 compared indicators, all three for gross capital formation, IN.3.2.4), but the file does not say which economy, and no list of values is published. Credit: "WIPO, Global Innovation Index 2026, CC BY 4.0"; the page says that WIPO is not responsible for FPR's adaptation. **Third-party data in the GII database are excluded from WIPO's licence** (most values come from the World Bank, UNESCO, the IMF and others, each under its own terms), which is why the economies' values are not republished and no economy is named.

## FPR's working file: what its formulas show

`Global Innovation Index - Back up Data.xlsx` was read on 2026-10-23 with openpyxl (`data_only=False`, read-only, never saved); only formulas and indicator values were read, and none of its personal data were copied. It holds 1,243 formulas, all of them computing **Puerto Rico's raw values**: ratios to PPP GDP or population (such as 108/126.158 patents per billion PPP$), shares of total trade from the Junta de Planificación's Table 18, three-year averages (FDI, IP payments and receipts), the PISA mean of the three scores, the productivity growth from Table 1 and BLS LAUS, the knowledge-intensive employment share from BLS OES (COUNTIF over the SOC codes), and the ICT access sub-index (SUMPRODUCT of ITU weights). 4.3.1 points at the United States' cell of the GII 2020 block. The GII 2020 block (`Analysis_…`: ranks, scores and values of the 131 economies) is pasted values, with **no formula**: the workbook has no normalization, no outlier treatment and no ranking formula. The printed ranks were therefore placed by hand: Puerto Rico's value set among the GII 2020 values (which reproduces 44 of the 56, see above). The block's scores confirm the GII 2020 method as the technical notes describe it: min-max with the sample's min and max for the untransformed indicators (within the rounding of the values shown), survey questions kept on their 1-7 range, and the inverted scale for the four * indicators.

## Changes in the index since 2020

The page's section 3 (loaded on demand) and `gii_crosswalk.json` hold the details, with sources (WIPO GII 2026 database and Appendix I; GII 2025 Appendix I; GII 2020 Appendices III and IV; all checked 2026-10-23).
- **Editions:** GII 2020, 131 economies, 80 indicators (53 input, 27 output); GII 2025, 139 economies, 78 indicators; GII 2026 (19th, 2026-09-29), 139 economies, 79 indicators (54 input, 25 output). The same 7 pillars and 21 sub-pillars.
- **The 80 indicators of 2020 in the GII 2026:** 49 the same (some renumbered), 8 renamed, 7 redefined (3.1.1 and 6.1.2 changed calculation in 2025, 3.1.2 is now WIPO's index of five ITU indicators, 4.2.3 changed provider, 6.2.1 is now a five-year average, 7.3.1 and 7.3.2 merged), 16 dropped (the five Doing Business indicators, two World Economic Forum questions, firms offering training, e-participation, the Environmental Performance Index, R&D financed from abroad, joint ventures, new business density, FDI outflows, printing and media, Wikipedia edits). 16 indicators are new, such as policy stability (WEF), entrepreneurship policy and finance (GEM), aviation dwell time, low-carbon energy, three PitchBook venture-capital indicators, youth demographic dividend, unicorn valuation, export complexity and GitHub commits; Puerto Rico data exist for two (UN WPP; GitHub Innovation Graph), FPR estimates one (low-carbon energy use, from the EIA, since 2026-10-29) and an FPR estimate is possible for one (the Economic Census), two do not cover Puerto Rico (WEF survey; LPI), five are proprietary, and five were not verified.
- **Method:** min-max normalization unchanged; the outlier rule now needs both skewness > 2.25 and kurtosis > 3.5 (2020: either); 23 winsorized and 9 log-transformed indicators in 2026 (24 winsorized in 2020); half weights for 27 indicators in 2020, only 1.2.1 and 1.2.2 in 2026 (and sub-pillars 7.2 and 7.3 in both); data generally from 2016 on; since 2024 utility models do not count toward the coverage minimum. WIPO warns that scores and ranks are not directly comparable between editions.
- **How the page shows it:** a badge on each row (Igual, Renombrado, Redefinido, Eliminado en el GII 2026); the open row names the GII 2026 code and what changed; a dropped indicator keeps its original and says "Eliminado en el GII 2026" in the updated cell.

- GII 2020: CC BY-NC-ND 3.0 IGO. Only Puerto Rico's values and FPR's printed ranks are shown; no other economy's GII 2020 data. An open licence review on `gii_profile.json` asks Compliance about the printed ranks (derived from GII 2020 data) and the three proprietary 2021 values.
- GII 2026: CC BY 4.0, third-party content excluded. World Bank WGI and WDI: CC BY 4.0. UNESCO UIS: CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO. IMF: free reuse with attribution. BLS (LAUS, OEWS), EIA and Census Bureau population estimates: US Government works (EIA and BLS ask to be acknowledged as the source).
- The workbook and the source folder hold names, emails and telephone numbers of external contacts and staff. None is copied: only indicator data and source URLs reach the repository.

## Update it

```
python gii.py --refresh                        # newest releases into cache/gii/, then site/data/gii_profile.json (downloads about 32 MB: INTL.zip and the OEWS file; no API key;
                                               #   set FRESHNESS_CONTACT to an FPR role mailbox if bls.gov refuses the OEWS file)
# a new GII edition: download its database into cache/gii/<year>/, point G26_* in gii.py at it, then
python -I gii.py --extract-2026                # with a Python that has openpyxl
python gii.py                                  # also writes site/data/gii_changes.json from gii_crosswalk.json
python dictionary.py; python catalog.py; python ledger.py --backfill origin/main; python registry.py
python -m unittest tests.test_gii
node tests/browser/gii_page.js http://127.0.0.1:8041 <shots folder>
```
