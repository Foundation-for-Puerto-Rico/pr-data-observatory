# Connections between municipios

HANDOFF decision 105 (2026-11-13). The project lead asked to check the transportation data of the observatory, find what else can be added "for the gap analysis we need to conduct", and "make visible the gaps where transportation routes can be added. Maybe this is a simulated layer that we can add in a knowledge area and connect the transportation systems between municipalities." This is step 1 of `docs/transportation-upgrade-2026-11.md`.

It sets two open datasets side by side: where people travel to work, and where a transit route is listed. Where many people travel between two municipios and no timetabled route joins them, it draws a simulated connection along the roads.

**It proposes nothing.** A simulated connection marks a place to look at. The transit analysis plan (`docs/transit-network-analysis-plan-2026-10.md`, decision 4) reserves a public recommendation of new routes or service changes for FPR's leadership, so the page, the layers, the dictionary and the glossary all say "a simulation, not a proposed route", and the tests check that they do.

US Government work. It is the newest release (the Bureau publishes one every five years; the folder list is checked every 90 days). ## How each figure is made

1. **Workers between two municipios.** The two directions are added. The margin of error of the sum is the root of the sum of the squares of the two margins (90 percent). A pair is `low` when its margin is as large as the estimate: 481 of the 1,084 pairs. They stay in the table, marked, and never get a simulated connection.
2. **Timetabled link.** A route in the weekday, Saturday or Sunday file with stops in both municipios (each stop in the municipio whose outline holds it, or the nearest outline for a point just outside every outline; `transport.json`'s own `municipality` gives the municipio with the nearest town centre in that case and put five stops of Cataño's waterfront, the Cataño terminal among them, in San Juan, which made San Juan–Toa Baja look timetabled through ATI route 37) (`path_stops` holds the stops of each trip pattern). Seven municipal systems (Bayamón, Caguas, Jayuya, Juncos, San Lorenzo, San Sebastián, Vega Baja) time only the first and last stop of some routes, so those patterns hold two stops: for a municipal route with no longer pattern, the operator's stops within 35 m of the route's line (`transit_routes.geojson`, drawn through the route's stops) count as its stops. The ATI's two-stop routes are express routes and are left as they are. Direct routes only. One stop just across a municipal line is enough, so the table gives the weekday trips. `current` when the operator's timetable still runs on the day `operators.json` was built: 23 pairs, nearly all around San Juan, plus Jayuya–Utuado, Dorado–Vega Alta, Morovis–Vega Baja, Cabo Rojo–Hormigueros, Canóvanas–Río Grande and the two ferries from Ceiba. Six more pairs (Caguas–Gurabo and five around Juncos and San Lorenzo) have only routes whose timetable expired. The first build read only the pattern stops and missed those seven pairs; a check of the stops by municipio and operator against the pairs found it.
3. **Público link (2020 list).** The rows of one code are one route (some routes are listed once for each direction, or in pieces): 334 routes. A route joins the municipios where its line ends (the two ends farthest apart) and those whose town centre it passes within 1 km. The first version counted every municipio with 500 m of the line inside it; that made a Mayagüez–Río Piedras route on the expressway "join" 19 municipios, so the rule was tightened. 147 routes join two municipios or more.
4. **Status of a pair**, the first that applies: `timetable` (22 pairs, 26.8% of the workers who cross a municipal line), `expired` (6 pairs, 2.2%), `publico` (167 pairs, 34.5%), `none` (889 pairs, 36.5%).
5. **Reachable with a transfer** (`via`). No direct route, but the two municipios are in the same group of the graph of current timetabled links (39 pairs, 52,638 workers). No journey time is computed.
6. **Road path.** The fastest path on `roads.bin` between the road nodes nearest the two town centres, at the route planner's speeds by kind of road, without traffic. Vieques and Culebra have no road to the main island: their 13 pairs have no distance.
7. **Simulated connection.** A pair with 500 workers or more, not `low`, and no timetabled route (current or expired): 182 pairs, 280,269 workers; 81 with no route listed and 101 with a público route on the 2020 list. The line is the road path, simplified to 12 m.
7b. **Line of a timetabled pair** (`timetabled_links.geojson`, the page's map only). The stretch of the pair's route with the most weekday trips, as `transit_routes.geojson` draws it, from its point inside each municipio (outline widened by 150 m, so a ferry terminal on the shore counts) nearest that town centre. A route that serves San Juan in Río Piedras is drawn to Río Piedras, not to Old San Juan. When no route of the pair has a line inside both municipios, the road path between the two centres; with the timetables of 2026-10-29 every pair has a route line (17 bus, 3 rail, 2 ferry). Never a straight line: until 2026-11-14 the page drew straight lines between town centres, which crossed the sea to Vieques and Culebra and did not show where the service runs.
8. **Municipios.** Workers who live there, who work elsewhere and who come in; the destination with the most workers; the share of the workers travelling in or out who do so between pairs with a timetabled route; the state of the timetable of the municipio's own system (`operators.json`).

## Checks

`tests/test_transit_links.py`: the público rules on made-up routes (ends and centres; a route that only crosses a municipio does not join it; rows of one code are one route); the published totals add up three ways (pairs, statuses, municipios); a simulated connection exists exactly where the rule says; the layer's lines carry the pairs' figures; the islands have no road distance; `--check` where the downloads are cached. `tests/transit_links_page.test.js`: the page's tally and filters against the file; a municipio's pairs hold everyone who leaves it or comes in; the layers' styles; "a simulation, not a proposed route" in the pop-up, the note, the help and the page.

Independent check, 2026-11-13: the Puerto Rico rows of the Census table were summed by hand in a separate script before the builder was written (532,691 workers between municipios in 1,626 one-way flows); the builder gives the same total. A sample of 60 pairs drawn with a seeded generator (60 distinct) matched the table's workers and margins, and the pairs with a timetabled route were recomputed from the stops of every pattern in a separate script (the same 22 before the route-line rule was added).

## Limits, stated on the page

- Only work trips, of 2016-2020, from a survey. Not trips to study, to a doctor or to shop, and not people without a job.
- Públicos run without timetables, and the list is of 2020: "no timetabled route" is not "no service". The first step for any pair is to confirm with the operators what runs today.
- A timetable is the service an operator publishes, not the service that ran; systems that publish none are missing.
- Town centres, not job centres: San Juan's centre is Old San Juan.
- A simulated connection has no stops, no frequency and no cost. Those belong to a transit study.

## Map layers

Both are in the shared list `core/facilities.js` (2D and 3D), group Infrastructure and mobility: `l=publicos` and `l=links`. A line layer can now be styled by class and by a number (`line: { prop, colors, labels, width, dash }`, `lineStyle`); the older line layer (`fprroutes`) is drawn as before. The simulated connections are dashed, orange (no route listed) or purple (a público route on the 2020 list), and wider where more workers travel.

## Traffic on its roads (step 2, 2026-11-13)

When `site/data/traffic_roads.geojson` exists (`traffic.py`, `docs/traffic.md`), `transit_links.py` adds to each simulated connection the traffic on the roads it follows: points every 100 m along the line take the annual average daily traffic of the counted main road within 25 m; `aadt_med` is their median, `aadt_max` the highest and `counted_pct` the share of points on a counted road. The page shows the median in the last column of the pairs table; the pop-up gives both. Run `traffic.py` before `transit_links.py` (the refresh unit `traffic` does). A point next to a crossing road can take that road's count, which can lift `aadt_max`; the median is little affected.

## Second pass of the gap analysis (step 5, 2026-11-14)

The project lead on 2026-11-14: "Sigue con paso 5." Step 5 of `docs/transportation-upgrade-2026-11.md` joins steps 1 to 4 with what the site already has. It adds facts to the page and the map layer; **it publishes no ranking or priority index** (the transit plan's decision 4 reserves a public recommendation of where service should go for FPR leadership; the barrio ranking of `docs/analysis/transit-needs-2026-11/` stays unpublished).

- **Time by timetable** (`tt_best`, `tt_typ`, `tt_xfers`, `tt_why` on every pair). `transit_times()` runs a connection scan over the weekday trips of `trips.json` (75,118 stop-to-stop connections): from each town centre, leaving every 15 minutes from 6:00 to 9:00, it walks up to 1 km to a stop with weekday service, rides, transfers on foot up to 400 m (straight line times 1.3 at 1.2 m/s, plus 2 minutes), and walks up to 1 km to the other centre; nothing over 3 hours. `tt_best` is the fastest of the 13 departures in either direction, `tt_typ` the median (the wait included), `tt_xfers` the transfers on the fastest. A pair with no trip says why: `tt_why` 1, a town centre has no stop of the weekday timetables within 1 km; 2, no trip within 3 hours.
  Result (timetables of weekday (typical Wednesday service)): 28 of the 1,084 pairs have a trip, all in and around San Juan; the fastest takes a median of 4.5 times as long as driving without traffic (Caguas–San Juan 103 minutes, 26 by car). 52 of the 78 town centres have no stop with weekday service within 1 km (`stop_km` on each municipio): most municipios publish no timetable, and some stops are far from the plaza (Ceiba's ferry terminal is 5 km away).
  Limits: municipal systems that time only the ends of their trips lose the trips that board or leave at their other stops; públicos are in no timetable; the trip is from plaza to plaza, not from where people live.
- **Need at each end** (`no_vehicle_pct`, `poverty_pct` on each municipio): households with no vehicle (B25044, `economy_series.json`) and residents below the poverty level (B17001, `housing_series.json`), ACS 2020-2024, as on the Analysis tab. Municipio level on purpose: barrio estimates have large margins (they are in the unpublished barrio ranking).
- **Along a simulated connection** (`along_res`, `along_far`, `bridges_poor`, `bridges_closed`): the 2020 Census residents of the blocks within 1 km of the line and those of them beyond 400 m in a straight line of a scheduled stop (`population_blocks.json`); the bridges of the National Bridge Inventory within 50 m of the line in poor condition or closed (`bridges.geojson`). 130 of the 182 connections pass a bridge in poor condition. Connections share roads: the counts must not be added up.

The page shows two new figure cards, a paragraph with the results, the new columns in both tables and three method notes; the map layer's pop-up shows the corridor and the time by timetable. The refresh unit `inventories` now rebuilds the links after the bridges. Checks: `tests/test_transit_links.py` (a direct trip and one with a transfer on a made-up network; the published figures are consistent).
