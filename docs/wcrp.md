## What it is

`site/wcrp.html` is the WCRP area of the Knowledge Area (the Housing page's sibling; both carry the `.ka-tabs` group: Housing, WCRP). In order (2026-10-19):

1. **About WCRP** (short): the program in the project lead's wording, the note "The program description is Foundation for Puerto Rico's." and counts of the 76 communities of `wcrp.geojson` (communities, municipios, approved plans) and of the barrios on the vulnerability map, computed from the files.
2. **1 · Social Capital map**: one plain sentence, then the themes of the original WCRP Social Capital Maps (health, education and culture, security, Comunidades Especiales, community services, environment) on one map with theme switches, drawn from the observatory's current official layers, and a municipio summary beside the vulnerability levels of its barrios (see "The Social Capital map"). The longer texts (the sources, the notes on the counts, the Comunidades Especiales list) are closed `<details>`.
3. **2 · Vulnerability map**: one plain sentence, then the 901 barrios shaded by their level (fifths, 1 low to 5 high) for an area (overall, economy, education, environment, health, housing, infrastructure) and a version: **Original 2022** (as published) or **Updated 2020–2024** (the same method on the latest survey and current local conditions). Beside the map on a wide screen and under it on a phone, one legend, **"What the levels mean"**, always visible: the five levels in the map's colours with the number of barrios at each (1 "among the 20% least vulnerable barrios", 5 "among the 20% most vulnerable barrios"), the no-value swatch ("No residents or no data"), one paragraph on what the index is and is not, a line on the area shown built from that version's inputs in plain words ("Economy. Looks at: poverty, public assistance and unemployment."; Overall: "Combines the six areas with equal weight."), a line on the version, and links to "Read with care" (a closed `<details>` right under the map) and to the comparison section. A municipio filter zooms and dims the others; the WCRP community points can be switched on. A barrio's pop-up gives its seven levels in both versions and the inputs of the area shown (loaded with the first click); with Overall, it also gives the barrio's age groups and landslide susceptibility, beside the index and never in it (see "Beside the index"). CSV of every barrio, both versions.
4. **How the index is built** (closed `<details>`): the method, the inputs of each area in both versions, what the updated version leaves out and why, what is shown beside the index (age groups, landslides) and why it is not an input, the check of the method against the original, the known errors.
5. **More about the comparison between 2022 and the updated version** (closed `<details>`): how many barrios change level, by area, why, and a button, "Show on the map the barrios whose level changes", that switches the map to the **change** view (the updated level minus the original, in the diverging colours) and scrolls to it. The change view is not in the version selector; while it is on, a bar above the map says so and its button goes back to the version selector ("Back to Original 2022 and Updated 2020–2024").
6. **Sources and licenses** (closed `<details>`).

The vulnerability map's area list, version selector (with the years, "Updated 2020–2024", from the start) and communities switch are set up by `wcrp.js` before the map's script arrives: a choice made then is kept and loads the map. If one of the Social Capital map's files fails, the map still draws its points and says what failed, and the vulnerability map still loads on approach (review of PR #69; `tests/browser/wcrp_page.js` blocks `care_series.json` to check it). In English, the 1 to 5 value is a **level** wherever a reader sees it (the legend, pop-ups, tooltips, the CSV's "level (1-5)" columns, the comparison, the method, the sources, the dictionary's names and summaries), as the Spanish "nivel"; "rank correlation" and the verb "rank" stay.

The view is kept in the address (`#a=health&v=rebuild&m=Adjuntas`); `v=change` opens the change view, so the old links keep working. An address with `a=` or `v=` loads the vulnerability map at once and scrolls to it. Every `<details>` is closed at first and has an id; a link to it or to anything inside it (the bar "On this page", the legend's links, an address such as `wcrp.html#method`, the data dictionary's links) opens it first (`reveal` in `wcrp.js`), and printing opens them all. Each `<summary>` holds a real heading (h2 to h4), so the page's outline and a screen reader's heading list keep them. `dictionary.py --links` checks that every `wcrp.html#part` link of the dictionary names an id of the page.

On the main map, the 14 indices are datasets of the Economy tab, group "Vulnerability (WCRP)" (see "On the main map").

## The original (as published)

Read live from the public ArcGIS REST services of the PlanningCDBG account (`https://services5.arcgis.com/phhDE88zbaXQ3N1m/arcgis/rest/services/`): `All_Indicator` (the OVERALL row of each barrio carries the six area indices, the overall index and their ranks) and `Master_Data_Table_Update` (the barrio input values; the municipio and Puerto Rico columns are dropped because several are mislabelled). Both were last edited in March 2022; `licenseInfo` and `accessInformation` are empty on both items. All 901 GEOIDs match `barrios.geojson`; the 14 barrios with no residents have no value (white paper, appendix B). The 21 barrios with nobody aged 3 to 24 (appendix C) carry a 0 for school enrollment in the service; the observatory stores no value, as the white paper says.

`refresh/sources.json` checks the services' last-edit date every 30 days (unit `wcrp`, run by hand and reviewed by a person).

**Refresh with the local conditions.** The rebuild reads `conditions_barrio.json`, which the `conditions` unit of `refresh/sources.json` rebuilds on a GitHub runner when one of its sources has a new release (`rebuild.yml`). That unit's run ends with `python wcrp_rvi.py`, and its files include `wcrp_rvi_barrio.json` and `wcrp_rvi_inputs.json`, so the WCRP files are rebuilt from the new conditions in the same pull request; without it, `tests/test_wcrp_rvi.py` and `wcrp_rvi.py --check` would fail on `main` until someone reran the script. A clean runner has no cache, so the run reads the original's ArcGIS services again and the ACS tables with `acs_small.py`; the new harvest date alone is not a change (`"ignore": ["harvested"]`). When the WCRP files change, the pull request waits for a person (`"human_if_changed"`, read by `freshness.py --review`), as every WCRP rebuild does; the reviewer also updates the figures this file and the dictionary entries quote (rank changes, counts). If the ArcGIS services do not answer, the conditions rebuild fails with them and is run again by hand (`docs/automation.md`). The survey's barrio file is rebuilt by hand (unit `housing`), and its instructions end with `python wcrp_rvi.py`. `tests/test_wcrp_rvi.py` (`RefreshCoupling`) checks the coupling: every runner unit that rewrites an input of the rebuild runs `wcrp_rvi.py` last and lists its two files for review, such a run waits for a person, and the published conditions inputs of the rebuild equal `conditions_barrio.json`.

## The rebuild

The white paper's method (Hunter College, December 2021) on ACS 2020–2024 and, for the inputs the survey does not hold, the observatory's local conditions (`conditions_barrio.json`, built by `conditions.py` from EPA, HUD CHAS, TIGER/Line and a drive time to a CMS general hospital; `docs/conditions.md`; decision 41):

1. **Percentile** of each input among the barrios that have a value: the share of them with a strictly lower value. This is the definition that gives the original back from its own inputs (below). Street intersections count the other way (fewer is more vulnerable): their percentile is taken on the negated density, as the original's (`FEWER_IS_WORSE`).
2. **Area**: the percentiles are added and the sum ranked again; infrastructure goes through two sub-indicators (telecommunications, transportation). Housing is the plain sum of its three inputs: the white paper describes two sub-indicators, but only the plain sum gives back the published housing index (below). A barrio that lacks an input gets the sum of the ones it has scaled to the full count: the white paper's education rule (no-diploma percentile doubled), applied to every area.
3. **Overall**: the percentile of the sum of the six areas, equal weights.
4. **Rank**: the fifth of the index. A barrio with no residents in the window has no value (16 in 2020–2024).

| Area | Inputs in the rebuild | Left out |
|---|---|---|
| Economy | poverty, households with cash public assistance (B19057), unemployment rate | none |
| Education | people aged 3 to 24 not enrolled (B14003), adults 25+ without a diploma | none |
| Environment | homes built before 1960 (lead-paint indicator, B25034); in a sulfur dioxide or lead non-attainment area (Green Book, flag); in a 303(d)-listed water assessment unit (ATTAINS 2024, flag)\*; active RMP facilities (FRS, count)\*; municipal solid waste landfill, open or closed (LMOP, flag)\*; Superfund NPL sites (FRS, count); TRI facilities (reporting year 2024, count) | solid waste facilities other than landfills (transfer stations, recycling, open dumps): no public source states terms (`wanted.json` `open-dumps`) |
| Health | disability, no health insurance; drive time to the nearest general hospital (estimate)\* | none |
| Housing | vacancy, homes built before 1990 (B25034); households with severe housing problems (HUD CHAS 2016–2020, tract counts spread to barrios: estimate)\* | none |
| Infrastructure | no computer (B28003), no internet access, no broadband (B28002); no vehicle; street intersections per square mile (TIGER/Line 2026)\* | none |

Variables already in `acs_barrio.json` are taken from it; the five other tables are read with `acs_small.py` for the same window; the rest come from `conditions_barrio.json`, each in its own source's vintage, so an older survey window gets the same values. A flag is 1 where the barrio's share of the area is above 0. No input is replaced by a proxy. Margins of error are not carried into the index.

\* **Defined differently from the original** (`REBUILD_NOTE`, shown under the page's inputs table with counts from `checks.definitions`):
- **Impaired waters.** Puerto Rico maps the ATTAINS units of its rivers as the land that drains to each assessed reach (catchments, not water bodies), so 772 of the 885 barrios ranked touch a 303(d)-listed unit, against 436 of 887 in the original. The flag separates barrios much less than the original's did.
- **Landfills.** LMOP lists municipal solid waste landfills only (32, open or closed); the original's "landfill or open dump" layer came from FPR. 32 barrios are flagged, 27 in the original.
- **RMP facilities.** Only the 33 facilities FRS marks active are counted; the original counted 149 in its barrios and does not say which.
- **Non-attainment.** Areas still in non-attainment (San Juan and Guayama-Salinas for SO₂, Arecibo for lead): 44 barrios flagged in both versions.
- **Drive time to a hospital.** The observatory's estimate along OpenStreetMap roads at free-flow speeds from each barrio's population-weighted point (the original used the centroid and does not say how it measured). **Vieques, Culebra and Isla de Mona** have no road to a hospital and no value (the original gave them 1 to 18 minutes, to a facility on the island). The rebuild ranks their health **without that input** (the missing-input rule: disability and insurance scaled to three inputs), for 11 barrios with residents; the page and the pop-up say so, and that this leaves out how far they are from hospital care.
- **Severe housing problems.** HUD publishes CHAS for tracts, not barrios: the barrio value is an estimate. Its rank correlation with the original's barrio values is 0.36.
- **Intersection density.** TIGER/Line 2026 nodes where three or more street edges meet, over land area; the original counted TIGER 2020 in a way it does not describe (mean 117 per square mile per barrio against 63 here). Only the order compares (rank correlation 0.96 with the original's values).

The original does not say which ACS cells it used, so survey definitions can differ too: households without a computer average 62% per barrio in the original and 20% in the rebuild.

## The version slider (2026-11-04)

The project lead asked for the version to be a slider "so I can see how it changes each year (with 2022 as the original)". The map's Version control is now a range input: **Original 2022**, then the updated version in every five-year window the survey publishes for barrios (2017–2021, 2018–2022, 2019–2023, 2020–2024; the last is the updated version). `wcrp_rvi.py` (`levels_by_window`) rebuilds each window with the same method; the inputs that do not come from the survey keep their own vintage, so only the survey moves. The file keeps the levels only (`series.levels`: one digit a barrio, 0 for none, in the order of `geo.ids`), so the pop-up's index value and inputs, the CSV and the comparison stay with the original and the latest window. The address keeps the stop (`#v=w2022`).

Windows that share years are not independent, and a note under the slider says so. Share of barrios whose overall level changes between neighbouring windows (`series.steps`): 2017–2021 to 2018–2022 35.2%, 2018–2022 to 2019–2023 38.3%, 2019–2023 to 2020–2024 34.3%. That is close to the 52% the comparison measures between two windows that share two years, so most of the year-to-year movement is survey error, not change on the ground.

## Beside the index: age groups and landslides (2026-10-18)

Main added the residents' age groups by barrio (PR #61: `acs_barrio.json` `acs_under18_pct`, `acs_age18to44_pct`, `acs_age45to64_pct`, `acs_age65plus_pct`, from B01001) and a current landslide susceptibility map (PR #61: `landslide_barrio.json`, USGS, Hughes and Schulz 2020). The rebuild takes an input only where the white paper's method has it, and it has neither:

- **Age.** The white paper's Appendix A lists "vulnerable populations (children, elderly, disabilities)" among the economy candidates, not among the inputs chosen; no area of the index uses age. The original dashboard showed each barrio's age groups beside the index as its "vulnerable population" (All_Indicator `UNDER17`, `OVER65` and the age bands, counts, ACS 2015–2019). The rebuild now shows the current survey's shares of the same groups (under 18, 18 to 44, 45 to 64, 65 and over, 2020–2024) with their margins of error, labelled as survey estimates. `wcrp_rvi.py` copies them from `acs_barrio.json` into `wcrp_rvi_inputs.json` (`rebuild.context`, `rebuild.context_moe`; `CONTEXT`); the labels and the note are in `wcrp_rvi_barrio.json` (`method.context`, `method.context_note`). The original's groups (under 17, over 65) do not match these bands exactly, so the page does not compare them.
- **Landslides.** The white paper says that risk variables such as flooding, landslides and earthquakes "should be contained in separate layers"; the original's landslide layer (the 1979 four-class map) was a service, not an input. The rebuild keeps it out. The pop-up shows the barrio's share of land highly susceptible and the share of residents living on it (an estimate), read from main's `landslide_barrio.json` on the first pop-up (19 KB compressed), never copied (`HAZARD_CONTEXT`; `method.hazards`). The 15 barrios of Vieques, Culebra and Isla de Mona say "not mapped (main island only)"; the three with no residents in 2020 say so.

**What changed, exactly.** No input, weight or rank of the index changed: the 14 indices are the same as before the merge, and `tests/test_wcrp_rvi.py` (`Context`) checks that no age or landslide variable is an input, that the age groups equal `acs_barrio.json` for the rebuild's window, and that the landslide values are read from main's file, not copied. The page gained the "Beside the index" card in section 2 and the context block of the Overall pop-up. The age groups follow `acs_barrio.json`, which the `housing` unit rebuilds by hand, and its instructions already end with `python wcrp_rvi.py`; the landslide values are read live, so the `landslide` unit needs no coupling.

## Checks (all measured by `wcrp_rvi.py` and kept in `checks`)

**The method against the original.** Applied to the original's own published inputs, the method gives back its published index with rank correlation 1.00 for overall, education, health, housing and infrastructure (98.9 to 99.8% of barrios keep their rank). It does not for economy (0.68) and environment (0.93); the white paper does not explain why. Housing reproduces as the flat sum of its three inputs (1.00), not as the two sub-indicators the white paper describes (0.96).

**A hypothesis for economy** (a reviewer's, verified 2026-10-17; `checks.reproduced.econ_income_as_higher_no_unemployment`): an index built from public assistance, poverty and per capita income, with higher income counted as more vulnerable and without unemployment, matches the published economy index much better (rank correlation 0.93 instead of 0.68; 81% of barrios keep their rank, against 39%). That would mean the published economy index counts income the wrong way round and leaves out unemployment. The page states it as a hypothesis to confirm with Hunter College; the rebuild keeps the method the white paper describes.

**How far the rebuild moves the ranks** (879 barrios ranked in both; recomputed 2026-10-18 after the merge of `main` with PRs #58 to #62, unchanged because no new layer enters the index): 67.9% change overall rank and 28.6% move two ranks or more (rank correlation 0.50). Before the new inputs (survey only): 68.1%, 30.5% and 0.46. By area, 64 to 73% change rank. Where it comes from:

| Area | Rebuild vs original: rank changes | moves 2+ | rank correlation | Original values, rebuild's inputs: rank changes | Rebuild on 2017–2021 vs 2020–2024: rank changes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Overall | 68% | 29% | 0.50 | 29% | 52% |
| Economy | 73% | 38% | 0.26 | 61% | 57% |
| Education | 68% | 36% | 0.30 | 1% | 61% |
| Environment | 69% | 37% | 0.39 | 33% | 46% |
| Health | 67% | 30% | 0.43 | 1% | 56% |
| Housing | 68% | 30% | 0.48 | 1% | 49% |
| Infrastructure | 64% | 28% | 0.54 | 1% | 52% |

So: with the new inputs, the original's own values ranked with the rebuild's inputs and method change 29% of overall ranks (49% before), almost all through economy and environment, which the method does not reproduce from their own inputs; education, health, housing and infrastructure now keep 99% of their ranks. The rest is newer sources with other definitions, five years of change, and noise: the rebuild on two survey windows that share two years (2017–2021, 2020–2024) still changes 52% of overall ranks (55% before). That comparison is lower partly by construction: the non-survey inputs are the same in both windows, so it now measures the survey's noise only. A barrio's rank should be read with that in mind; groups of barrios are steadier.

**Known errors of the original**, disclosed on the page: the dashboard's municipio and Puerto Rico comparison columns are mislabelled (economy: unemployment shown as public assistance, public assistance as poverty, poverty as unemployed; the Puerto Rico column shows 5.2%, the public assistance share, as poverty, which is 44.1%; housing's municipio column is shifted); the municipio lead-paint values run from 11.1% to 264.2% (3 above 100%); homes built before 1990 reach 100.1% in 2 barrios.

`tests/test_wcrp_rvi.py` checks the method on a hand-worked fixture, that the original's ranks are fifths and equal its published ranks, that every one of the 901 barrios matches `barrios.geojson`, that each rebuild input maps to the original's column and the flags follow `conditions_barrio.json`, that fewer intersections rank as more vulnerable, that Vieques and Culebra are ranked in health without the hospital input, the economy hypothesis, the refresh coupling (above) and the hold. `tests/core.test.js` checks the engine side.

## The Social Capital map

The original WCRP Social Capital Maps (a PRDOH and FPR tool, CDBG-DR) showed community assets in six themes. The page rebuilds that view from the observatory's own layers, never from the 2019 SCM spreadsheets: each layer is the most current one, from a service the observatory refreshes.

| Theme | On the map | In the municipio summary |
|---|---|---|
| Health | nothing: health facility and treatment facility points are not published (HRSA site points stay local; SAMHSA is counts only) | hospitals and dialysis facilities (CMS, `care_series.json`), health center sites (HRSA, `health_series.json`), substance use and mental health treatment facilities (SAMHSA N-SUMHSS 2024, `community_series.json` `bh_all`; counted, not drawn) |
| Education and culture | public libraries (USAC E-Rate, `libraries.geojson`); historic places (NPS National Register, `historic_places.geojson`: the 269 listings drawn, 277 points; the 73 private homes and haciendas are not in the file) | libraries (`facility_series.json`); places on the National Register (`community_series.json` `nrhp_places`, 342, private homes counted) |
| Security | fire and EMS stations (USGS), police stations (OpenStreetMap) | the same counts |
| Community services | public housing developments (HUD); city halls (OpenStreetMap, `town_halls.geojson`, 65 features) | developments and units; city halls mapped, with annexes, counted from `town_halls.geojson` (no count for the 16 municipios with none mapped: they have a city hall that OpenStreetMap lacks); community water systems outside PRASA and the residents they serve (EPA ECHO, `infrastructure_series.json` `water_nonprasa`, `water_nonprasa_pop`; counted, not drawn) |
| Comunidades Especiales | the 713 polygons (`comunidades.geojson`); the pop-up adds the WCRP list's asset flags | communities and their housing units (2006); the list's flags |
| WCRP communities | the 76 points (`wcrp.geojson`, already on the page) | communities and approved plans |
| Environment | protected areas (`protected.geojson`, PAD-US) | residents inside a protected area (`exposure_municipio.json`, 2020 Census blocks) |

- **Reuse, not copies.** The points and areas are the 2D map's files (`facilities.py`, `community_assets.py`, `comunidades.py`, PAD-US), with the same colours, pop-up (name, type, municipio, listing date for historic places, source with its date) and "drawn of counted" note; `facilities.js` itself is tied to the main map, so `wcrp_social.js` reads its files and its Spanish (the module's scopes are `facilities` and `wcrp_social`). Every vector layer shares one canvas, so a click finds the shape on top.
- **With the page** (since 2026-10-19, when it became the first map; before, it loaded on approach). The page asks at once for its own files (`meta.json`, `wcrp_rvi_barrio.json`, `wcrp.geojson`, `wcrp_social.json`), the summary files (`facility_series.json`, `care_series.json`, `health_series.json`, `exposure_municipio.json`, `community_series.json`, `infrastructure_series.json`, `town_halls.geojson`) and the default themes' points (libraries, historic places, fire and EMS, police, public housing; city halls come with the summary files), all in parallel; the page is ready when the points are drawn. The barrio outlines (`barrios.geojson`, 265 KB) load only to zoom to a municipio, or with the vulnerability map. Comunidades Especiales (149 KB) and protected areas (414 KB) load only when switched on. The summary's levels by municipio come from the index file and the barrios' GEOIDs (a barrio's GEOID starts with its municipio's), not from the outlines.
- **One municipio for the page.** The summary's municipio and the vulnerability map's filter are the same (`#m=`): picking one in either updates both. The summary gives, for each asset type, the count, the rate per 1,000 residents (residents: the latest Census Bureau estimate, 2025; residents inside protected areas: the 2020 Census) and Puerto Rico's rate; beside it, for each of the seven areas, how the municipio's barrios spread over the five ranks in the original and in the rebuild (no rank is invented for a municipio). WCRP communities are listed as counts, not rates. A CSV gives every municipio.

- **Added 2026-10-18** (the themes of the original tool that main now holds, PR #58): historic and cultural places under Education and culture, city halls and non-PRASA community water systems under Community services, treatment facilities under Health. Sensitive places are counted, not drawn: treatment facilities (sensitive services), water systems outside PRASA (names are often a person's or a farm's) and historic places that are private homes. Each has its count and its rate per 1,000 residents in the summary and the CSV, beside Puerto Rico's rate. Puerto Rico: 342 National Register listings, 87 treatment facilities, 207 water systems outside PRASA serving 100,743 people, 65 city hall features in 62 municipios. `tests/test_wcrp_social.py` (`NewThemes`) checks that the page reads main's files and draws no file of a sensitive place.

### Community-asset flags of the Comunidades Especiales

The WCRP Social Capital Mapping list (`COMUNIDADES ESPECIALES.csv`, Community Resiliency Planning SharePoint) records, for 711 Comunidades Especiales, whether each has a community center, a library, an electronic library, a passive park, a sports court, a residents' association, a recreation association, a crime-prevention association and a PDI (the list's own column name; it does not define it). **Vintage: unknown; the WCRP Social Capital Mapping list, about 2019–2021, from the JP inventory.** No live source exists, so the repository keeps a cleaned extract, `wcrp_ce_assets.csv`, with only the match keys and the flags; the list's two hazard columns are left out (the polygons have the Junta's own). The list holds no personal data.

- **The copy.** The Microsoft 365 connector returned the file with its accented letters lost. Municipio names are restored from the 78 official names; a community or barrio name gets each lost letter from the polygon it matches when the two align letter by letter, or from the only word of the Junta's names that fits; 9 names keep a `?` for a letter nothing tells.

Puerto Rico: community center 243, library 39, electronic library 88, passive park 130, sports court 378, residents' association 480, recreation association 163, crime-prevention association 74, PDI 593 (about 111 to 127 rows have no information for each flag except PDI).

## On the main map

`js/core/datasets.js` adds the file with the existing adapters: `snapshotFile` (the barrio ids match no municipio, so every municipio has no value, and `noIsland` gives Puerto Rico none) and `levelFile` (the barrio level). The barrio outlines come from `barrios.geojson`; `acs_barrio.json` is not loaded for these layers. Main's local conditions (PR #56) reach the barrios another way, `barrioFile` (their barrio file loads on first use); `econ_level.js` reads either: a dataset with `level` brings its barrio values with it, one with `barrioFile` names its file, the rest use `acs_barrio.json`. The CSV by area of the dataset window stays for the survey's own barrio files (`adapters.levelsFor`); the WCRP page has its own CSV. The area profiles and the 3D view skip the file: they show municipios only. The 3D view loads with `datasets.load(..., ['wcrp'])`; the area profile, which since main's PR #64 fetches its first field's files (`FIRST`) and then the others (`datasets.fetchFiles`), leaves `wcrp` out of both (`LEFT_OUT` in `profile.js`; `tests/core.test.js`).
