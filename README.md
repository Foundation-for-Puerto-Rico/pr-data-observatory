# PR Data Observatory

A free, open model of Puerto Rico. It brings public information from many fields onto one map and one timeline, so you can compare and combine it: rent against income, flood zones against where people live, transit access against jobs, and how each has changed. Built by Foundation for Puerto Rico as a tool for public use and benefit. Open and public data only. The site needs no API keys or logins; the build uses one free NASA Earthdata login, for night-time lights, kept only as a GitHub secret (HANDOFF decision 75; nine sources state no licence: the dictionary says which). No personal data is knowingly published: the place listings carry business names but no street address, website or other contact detail, and private homes, housing, listings named by an address and care services are counted but not drawn (`docs/places-privacy.md`).

The fields ("domains") are defined in `dictionary_src/domains.json` and grow as data are added; `docs/domains.md` lists the current set.

**About "HANDOFF decision N".** This README, the guides in `docs/` and comments in the code cite numbered decisions in `HANDOFF.md`. That file is Foundation for Puerto Rico's internal decision log and is not part of this repository. What each decision changed is described where it is cited and in the guides in `docs/`.

# Observatorio de Datos de PR (PR Data Observatory)

Un modelo abierto y gratuito de Puerto Rico. Reúne información pública de muchos campos en un mismo mapa y una misma línea de tiempo, para que pueda compararla y combinarla: el alquiler frente al ingreso, las zonas inundables frente a dónde vive la gente, el acceso a transporte frente a los empleos y cómo ha cambiado cada cosa. Creado por Foundation for Puerto Rico como herramienta para el uso y beneficio del público. Solo datos abiertos y públicos. El sitio no necesita claves de API ni cuentas; la compilación usa una cuenta gratuita de NASA Earthdata, para las luces nocturnas, guardada solo como secreto de GitHub (decisión 75 de HANDOFF; nueve fuentes no declaran licencia: el diccionario dice cuáles). No se publican datos personales a sabiendas; los lugares incluyen el nombre, la dirección y el sitio web de cada negocio tal como los publica Overture Maps, y hay una revisión abierta de esos campos (`HANDOFF.md`, decisión 19).

**Sobre "decisión N de HANDOFF".** Este README, las guías en `docs/` y los comentarios del código citan decisiones numeradas de `HANDOFF.md`. Ese archivo es la bitácora interna de decisiones de Foundation for Puerto Rico y no forma parte de este repositorio. Lo que cambió cada decisión se describe donde se cita y en las guías de `docs/`.

**What it holds today** (it grows as data are added): tourism places (Overture Maps) · FEMA flood zones · NOAA sea level rise scenarios (live) · USGS protected areas · 2020 Census municipalities and population · mobility (ATI transit routes and stops, OpenStreetMap stops, stations, terminals, taxi stands, EV charging, FAA airports, Uber service listing) · per-municipality exposure and access analytics.

Companion to [VIEWPR](https://github.com/Foundation-for-Puerto-Rico/viewpr). VIEWPR stays as is; this is a separate artifact with its own data pipeline.

## What it answers

- What is where, and how much?
- How do places compare?
- What changed since a year?
- What do two things have in common, and what does that not prove?
- What is exposed to a hazard?
- What is missing from the data?

## The landing view and the visitor-economy module

The map opens on Puerto Rico with the municipal boundaries only. The **Overview** tab shows one headline figure for each field of the registry, with its trend where there is one; **Show on map** puts the figure's data on the map. The **Catalog** tab finds anything the dictionary documents (filters: field, geography level, year, licence and source) and adds it to the map. Both are generated from `dictionary_src/domains.json` and `site/data/catalog.json` (`python catalog.py`), so a new domain or dataset appears with no change to the page.

Tourism places are one module, the **visitor economy**. The 32,541 place listings (`places.json`, about 2.4 MB compressed with its access file; 1,005 holiday rentals and rented units among them are counted but not drawn, and homes, housing and care services filed under tourism categories are left out entirely, `docs/places-privacy.md`) and their access columns (`place_access.json`) are not part of the first load: the map fetches them the first time the *Tourism places* switch is turned on, a municipality's places are downloaded or the listings are searched. Until then the Municipality tab shows the figures stored for the default filters. The questions this module answers:

- What tourism supply does each municipality have, by category and type?
- How much of it sits inside a FEMA Special Flood Hazard Area?
- What would a 1 to 10 ft sea level rise reach?
- Which attractions sit inside protected lands?
- How much of the supply is reachable by transit (within a 400 m walk of a stop, station or terminal)? Where are the Tren Urbano, bus, ferry and airport hubs?
- Supply per 1,000 residents and per km², ranked across the 78 municipios.

## Transit network and what is live

The **Transit** tab of the 2D map is a planning view of every public transit system in Puerto Rico:

- **Operators.** 44 entries: ATI, AMA, the públicos and ferry authorities and 39 municipal systems (every agency that reports to the federal transit database), plus the OpenStreetMap routes, each with the state of its public data: 17 current timetables, 1 expiring, 7 expired, 16 with nothing public (2026-10 snapshot). Timetable status is worked out from the feed's end date every time the page opens.
- **Routes and stops** of ATI and 24 municipal systems from their public GTFS feeds, plus 16 públicos and trolley routes mapped in OpenStreetMap. Feeds without route shapes are **snapped to the road network**: each stop-to-stop leg follows the fastest road path (`roads.py` builds the graph from OpenStreetMap, `network.py` does the snapping). A feed that lists its stops out of order (Patillas lists them alphabetically) gets a visit order inferred from road distances and is drawn dashed, and its times are left out of the timetable replay. Stops whose longitude lost its minus sign (Jayuya's feed) are corrected on read.
- **Live data, stated plainly.** Real-time bus positions are **not public**: ATI lists GTFS-Realtime as "en desarrollo". The Transit tab shows what was checked (AMA Móvil, the independent transPRte app, ferries, municipal systems, ADS-B aircraft) and what each one needs. "Vehicles on their timetable" is a schedule calculation, labelled as such; weekday, Saturday and Sunday timetables are used according to Puerto Rico time. `OUTREACH_TRANSIT_DATA.md` has draft requests to ATI and to the municipalities with no feed.
- **Nightly refresh.** `.github/workflows/refresh-transit.yml` runs `refresh_network.py` every night: it re-reads the registry of municipal feeds, downloads every feed, and republishes only if something changed. A municipality that uploads a new feed appears the next day. The OpenStreetMap routes are refreshed by a local full build only: the Overpass servers refused GitHub's runners when this was tested (2026-10-02), so the nightly job keeps the last build's OSM routes. The job also refuses to publish if fewer than 20 operators return a feed.
- **Planning tools.** Residents within 400 m of a scheduled stop (2020 Census blocks) per municipality and overall; service gaps (blocks more than 800 m from any stop); integration points (stops of two systems within 250 m); and a **route planner**: name a start, an end and any stops between (a town, barrio, street, PR route number, landmark, transit stop or tourism place), or choose or drag points on the map; the route follows roads (the same router as the build, running in the browser) and is scored: length and indicative running time, residents and tourism places reached, residents with no scheduled stop today, overlap with existing routes, flood and protected-area exposure, municipalities crossed, connections at the ends, with GeoJSON export and a shareable link (`#r=lat,lng;lat,lng`). Both the 2D map and the 3D view have it.
- **Place search.** One box on both maps searches municipalities, tourism places, transit stops and routes, and an offline index of OpenStreetMap names (`gazetteer.py` writes `site/data/gazetteer.json`: barrios, towns, neighbourhoods, streets, PR route numbers, hospitals, schools, parks, beaches, peaks, landmarks). Matching ignores accents and expands `Av.`, `Carr.` and `PR 52`. Nothing typed leaves the page unless the visitor picks "Look up as an address online", which sends that text to OpenStreetMap's Nominatim service.

## Analysis (Tools) and the chat

**The 2D map's tabs** (navigation plan, HANDOFF decision 81): **Explore** (ten topics, each with ready-made views, its datasets, points and lines and FPR's analyses; the map layers; the Catalog), **Place** (municipality and region), **Tools** (Rankings of any dataset, Transit, Analysis) and **About**. The **On the map** panel lists every layer that is on, with its legend and year, whatever tab switched it on. **Ask** is on every page.

**Analysis** (Tools, the former Economy tab). Put as many datasets on the map at once as you like: shaded layers for rates and levels (two shaded layers together become a bivariate map), sized symbols for counts and dollars, each with its own normalisation (per 1,000 residents, per km², share of Puerto Rico, share of the municipality's jobs, location quotient, index, change since a base year), inflation adjustment for dollars (US CPI-U; Puerto Rico has none), natural/quantile/equal classes, and colours held fixed across years. One year control and play button drive every layer. Click the map for every visible layer's value at that place (with rank and margin of error); the **workbench** under the map has an inspector, a sortable table with CSV, a scatter plot with correlation, and a trend chart. Each dataset opens its definition, years and **source links**. The layers, year and settings live in the page address (`#e=...&ey=...`) so a view can be shared. Six ready-made views start the exploration.
- **Data** (`economy_series.py` → `site/data/economy_series.json`, 0.5 MB, 70 datasets, no API key): BLS QCEW jobs, establishments, wages, average pay, 19 industries, tourism jobs, industry concentration, 2014-2025; BLS LAUS unemployment and labor force 2010-2025 (no 2020 annual average in the file); ACS inequality, income bands, worker class, commuting, vehicles and internet 2010-2024 (with margins of error); USAspending federal awards by type 2008-2025; OpenFEMA Public Assistance by declaration year; jobs per employed resident. The Housing page series and the tourism-place counts join as datasets of their own, so housing and economy layers can sit on one map (108 in all).
- **Limits to keep in mind**: jobs are counted at the workplace and survey figures at the residence; jobs data withhold small cells and miss informal work; municipal federal-award totals overstate San Juan (statewide awards are booked there); no municipal GDP or commuter-flow data exist for Puerto Rico; ACS dollars are in the final year of each five-year window; Puerto Rico's CPI is approximated by the US index.

**The layer engine.** One set of shared scripts, `site/js/core/` (`docs/engine.md`), decides how a dataset becomes a layer on the 2D map (Explore, Rankings, Analysis), the Housing page and the 3D view: which year it shows (a survey year is written as its five-year window), how it is rescaled (per 1,000 residents, per 1,000 housing units, per km², share, index, change, percentile), how it is classed (quantiles, equal intervals, natural breaks, or four limits you type) and coloured (`docs/palettes.md` has the colour-vision test), and how it is described in a legend, a pop-up and a table. A value that is **less reliable** (a margin of error above 30% of the estimate, or a rate built on fewer than 10 counted) is hatched on the map, italic in the pop-up and listed in the legend; the Layers tab can also draw the tourism places that pass its filters as **hexagons of equal area** instead of pins.

**Ask the observatory** (chat, top bar). Ask in English or Spanish: what data exists, where something is highest, how two things relate, how something changed, what stands out in a municipality, what patterns are strongest, what is missing. It finds the datasets, computes the answer from the real numbers, puts the right layers on the map and says which year and which unit it used. It is **not** a language model: every sentence is built from computed facts, nothing typed leaves the browser, and it reports patterns, not causes. Questions it cannot answer go on a local wishlist (copy it or open it as a GitHub issue, after review). A model back end can be switched on later (`TWIN_CHAT.endpoint`, see the end of `site/js/chat.js`) using the same tools; that needs a small proxy FPR hosts, because no key may live on the site.
- **The database grows.** Every harvested dataset stays and new ones are added, in compliance: licence, source link and terms are recorded for each. `python registry.py` (also run by `build.py`) writes `site/data/registry.json`: every published file, its size, and where the total stands against GitHub Pages' limits (1 GB site, 100 MB file; planning starts at 60%, now about 7%). `site/data/wanted.json` lists 101 candidate datasets (jobs by industry and size, hazards and risk, schools and health, power and water, tourism demand, building footprints, recovery money and more) with source, licence, cadence, why it matters and what to check first; the chat reads it to answer what is missing. When the data outgrow the host, large files move to object storage as Parquet or PMTiles queried in the browser, while the small summaries stay on the page.

## Data dictionary and About

**`dictionary.html`** is the observatory's data dictionary: one interactive page for every dataset, variable, layer, table and measure. Each entry says what it means in plain words, the exact definition, the unit, geography and years, how the observatory computes it, how to read it, specific cautions, coded values, its sources (with licence and credit line), where it appears in the observatory (with links), related entries, and a **Cite** button that writes a credit line, APA 7, Chicago and BibTeX citation (naming both the observatory snapshot and the original source). Series entries download all years as CSV. The page also holds **Sources and licences** (moved from the About tab: publisher, licence as the source states it, credit line, how the observatory gets the data, cadence, checks), a **glossary**, a summary of the **published data files** that links to the **Data management** page (`data-management.html`: every file with its records, fields, values, size, first-published and last-updated dates, the dictionary items that document it and its compliance review, a growth chart and the counts of what is not published; `docs/data-management.md`), and **How to cite**. Search, filters (area, source, kind, time, margins of error), deep links (`dictionary.html#economy.qcew_jobs`) and a print stylesheet are built in. Dataset dialogs on the Economy tab and the Housing page and the chat link to their entries.
- **Source of truth:** `dictionary_src/` (entries per area in `entries/*.json`, `sources.json`, `glossary.json`, rules in `dictionary_src/README.md`). `python dictionary.py` writes `site/data/dictionary.json` and the smaller files the page reads (`dictionary_list.json` first, `dictionary_detail_<domain>.json` when an entry is opened); `python dictionary.py --coverage` lists published fields that no entry documents; `build.py` runs it. **Every new dataset or field needs an entry**, which keeps the dictionary complete as the database grows.
- The About tab on the map and in 3D now explains what the observatory is, who it is for, how to use it, what to keep in mind, privacy and how to cite, and says it is developed by Foundation for Puerto Rico as a tool for public use and benefit.

## Domains and the data contract

The observatory is a general model of Puerto Rico, not a tourism tool. Its fields of information are **domains**, an open set defined in one file, `dictionary_src/domains.json` (Spanish and English names and descriptions, icon, accent, headline indicator, example questions, status). `docs/domains.md` lists the current set and is generated; nothing in the pages or the documentation may hard-code a domain name or how many there are. Every dictionary entry carries a primary `domain` and a `domains` list (primary first, then secondary domains); `docs/tagging-review-2026-10.md` shows how the first 594 entries were tagged.

- **Data contract.** `dictionary_src/datasets.json` has one record per file in `site/data/` (domains, geography level and join key, time type, sources, licence class, cadence, size tier, open reviews). `python contract.py` checks the registry and the records against the files; `build.py` runs it at the end and **fails the build** on any violation (unregistered file, unknown domain or source, licence class laxer than the sources, unmatched join keys, years that disagree with the file, file over its size tier, personal or business contact data). Open reviews (personal data, licence, federal-grant compliance) are listed at every run: `python contract.py --reviews`.
- **Geography.** `docs/geography.md`: the crosswalk of areas (tourism regions, municipios, barrios, census tracts, block groups), how to join data to them, how to move numbers between barrios and tracts, and how to read small-area survey estimates.
- **Exposure series.** `docs/exposure.md`: how many residents and housing units in each municipio, barrio and census tract are inside a FEMA flood hazard area, inside the area flooded by 1 to 10 ft of sea level rise, inside a protected area, or within 400 m of a transit stop (`exposure_municipio.json`, `exposure_barrio.json`, `exposure_tract.json`), counted from census blocks; how it was checked, how to read it, and how to add a layer.
- **Health.** `docs/health.md`: health center sites and shortage-area designations for each municipio from HRSA's open data downloads (`health_series.json`, 11 datasets, built by `health_series.py`): what is in, what is left out and why (the Area Health Resources File needs HRSA's permission), how a figure is made, and the check against HRSA's own totals.
- **Small-area survey series.** `docs/small-area.md`: American Community Survey estimates with margins of error for 901 barrios, 981 census tracts and 2,555 block groups (`acs_barrio.json`, `acs_tract.json`, `acs_blockgroup.json`, read only when a person asks for a level) and the Population domain's series for the municipios (age, sex, household type, veterans, disability: `population_series.json`); how rough each variable is (measured), how it was checked, how to add a variable or a window.
- **Adding data.** `docs/adding-data.md` is the placement guide and checklist (does it fit a domain, how to create one, steps from source check to page weight).
- **Tests.** `python -m unittest discover -s tests` (the validators, and an acceptance test that a new domain is one registry object); it also runs `node --test tests/core.test.js` (the shared engine) when Node is installed. `tests/browser/` is a regression harness for a browser (captures of every page before and after a change, compared exactly; see its README).
- **Opening state.** The map opens with only the municipal boundaries on; every other layer and shading is off. The URL (`#l=places,flood,...&s=...`) restores any state.

## Spanish and English

The pages speak Spanish and English, Spanish first. The language comes from `?lang=es|en` in the address, else the choice made with the **ES | EN** switch in the top bar, else the browser (any Spanish in its language list gives Spanish; only English gives English), else Spanish; the switch reloads the page and keeps the map view (it lives in the URL hash). The code keeps its English strings, each wrapped in `T()`, `Tn()` or `Th()`; the Spanish lives in `i18n_src/es/<scope>.json` and `python i18n.py` builds one bundle per page, `site/i18n/es.<page>.js` (English loads nothing). `python i18n.py --check` (the build runs it) fails when a script asks for a string that has no Spanish, when a Spanish text loses a `{placeholder}` or an HTML tag, or when a data label has no Spanish. Rules for code and the Spanish style guide and terminology: `docs/i18n.md`.
Not translated yet, and said so on the page: the long text of the data dictionary entries, the series definitions and the source notes (the registry's domain names and descriptions are in both languages). Spanish drafted by Claude needs review by a native speaker before it is treated as final community-facing text.

## FPR Research in Data

`housing.html` is the first study in the **FPR Research in Data** series: Foundation for Puerto Rico publications turned into live visualizations. *The State of Housing in Puerto Rico* (2023) is shown table by table as published (Tables 2, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 and the headline figures) with a live version beside each, and every variable is extended to all 78 municipalities and Puerto Rico **for every year the public sources allow (2010 to 2025)**, with no API key:

- **Year bar.** One control moves every map, chart, table and the municipality profile to the same year (Latest, or any year 2010-2025); a play button steps through the years; a second control sets the base year for the change maps (default 2012, the study's baseline). Map colours are fixed across years so a colour means the same thing in every year. The year and base year are kept in the page address (`?y=2022&c=2012`).
- **Full data for every dataset.** Each map, table, KPI, profile row and catalog entry opens a dialog with all municipalities (and Puerto Rico) by year, a sortable table, a trend chart for any ticked municipalities, margins of error where the Census Bureau publishes them (a toggle; unreliable cells are italic), the definition, source and table IDs, and a CSV download. A data catalog at the end of the page lists all 46 series plus the published tables. `?d=median_gross_rent` opens a dataset directly.
- **Data** (`housing_series.py` → `site/data/housing_series.json`, 0.5 MB): resident population 2010-2025 (2010 and 2020 census counts, Population Estimates Vintage 2020 for 2011-2019 and Vintage 2025 for 2021-2025; the series steps up in 2020 because the old estimates were never revised to the 2020 count) and the 65+ share; census-day housing units, occupied and vacant for 2010 and 2020 (PL 94-171 table H1); ACS 5-year estimates for every window 2006-2010 to 2020-2024 (24 tables: housing stock, vacancy by type including the 'other vacant' abandoned-housing proxy, rental and homeowner vacancy rates, overcrowding, structure type and age, households, incomes by tenure, rents, values, owner costs, cost burden including severe burden, poverty, employment, education), each with its margin of error, plus the Table 6 affordability method for every year; and FEMA Individual Assistance households and dollars by municipality and disaster year (OpenFEMA). 2024 values match the previous pipeline's published figures exactly.
- **Updating.** `python housing_series.py` (also run by `build.py`) downloads only what is missing (the ACS files are read only as far as Puerto Rico's rows, so a fresh build takes about ten minutes and the cache stays small), and looks for the next ACS window and population-estimate vintage by itself; run it again when the Census Bureau publishes them (population estimates each spring, ACS 5-year each December: 2021-2025 is due December 2026). HUD's LIHTC, Fair Market Rent and Income Limits files were not added: HUD's site refuses automated downloads, so Table 10 stays as published.

Housing indicators also appear in the observatory's municipality panel, choropleths, rankings and the 3D columns (those still use the latest ACS window).

## Knowledge Area · WCRP (published, marked "In review")

The top bar's **Knowledge Area** holds FPR research and program tools as tabs: Housing (`housing.html`) and **WCRP** (`wcrp.html`). The WCRP area shows, first, the Social Capital map (community assets by theme, with a municipio summary), then the Risk and Vulnerability Indicators that Hunter College built for FPR's Whole Community Resilience Planning program, by barrio, with a legend of what levels 1 to 5 mean: the original as published in 2022 (read live from its public ArcGIS services) and "Updated 2020–2024", the same method on ACS 2020–2024 and current local conditions. The method, the inputs the updated version lacks, the comparison of the two versions, a check of the method against the original and the original's known errors are collapsible sections. The project lead approved the page for publication on 2026-10-19 and asked to keep its "In review" marker: the tab carries the small badge, the page shows one notice at the top and stays `noindex` while the marker is on, its Spanish is released, and the files' licence reviews stay open in the data contract until PRDOH confirms re-use. Guide: `docs/wcrp.md`.

## Knowledge Area · Public spending (published, marked "In review")

`public-spending.html` (area `publicspending`, under the Public money topic) shows what the Government of Puerto Rico and the federal government spend, by function. It keeps the "In review" notice and `noindex` until leadership has seen it (HANDOFF decision 73 (7)). Seven sections:

1. **The budget**: the Commonwealth budget from OGP's data sheets (`ogp_budget.py` → `spending_state.json`) as a treemap by function and agency, approved against spent, and the proposed, approved and spent pipeline by fiscal year.
2. **Where the money comes from**: funds of every budget, and bands from fund to function.
3. **What it pays for**: concepts of expense.
4. **Capital projects by municipio**: the Planning Board's four-year program (`pica.py` → `spending_pica.json`), on the outline map or as equal hexagons, with the money that names no municipio beside it.
5. **Federal money**: USAspending obligations by function, award type and program (`federal_awards.py` → `spending_federal.json`).
6. **One sector across the sources**: for a function, the budget, federal obligations, capital projects and dollars per resident side by side; for Education, spending per public school student and an estimate by municipio, labelled as an estimate.
7. **Disaster recovery money by municipio**: subawards of the Department of Housing's CDBG-DR and CDBG-MIT grants by municipio (`cdbg_dr.py` → `spending_cdbgdr.json`), with the prime grants and the money that names no municipio beside the map.

Each data section says when its file was built and is marked when the file is older than two of its update cycles. The sources are never added together: the rules (fiscal-year bases, stages, dedup groups, one population basis) are in `spending.py` and `docs/spending.md`; the function of each budget line comes from `dictionary_src/spending_crosswalk.csv`, a draft under review.

## Two views

- **`index.html` — 2D analysis.** Leaflet over an OpenFreeMap vector base map of OpenStreetMap data (`site/js/core/basemap.js`, `docs/basemap.md`): layers, municipality profiles, rankings, CSV export.
- **`3d.html` — 3D + Time.** Search box, route planner with a fly-along camera, and view controls (rotate, tilt, look straight down, home, find my location, keyboard help; keys N, T, H, Q, E and /). MapLibre GL with 3D terrain (Mapzen/AWS terrain tiles), OpenStreetMap building heights, terrain-draped flood zones and Comunidades Especiales, WCRP community points, sea level rise by year and NOAA scenario, a replay of every weekday transit trip (deck.gl TripsLayer), and hurricane tracks since 1851 (NOAA HURDAT2). Three clocks on one time bar: **Day** (00:00–24:00 schedule), **Years** (2020–2150 sea level), **Storms** (1851–2025).

Both views read the same `site/data/` files. No API keys anywhere: vector tiles from OpenFreeMap, terrain from the AWS Open Data terrain tiles, NOAA tiles streamed directly.

## Run it

```bash
python -m venv venv
venv\Scripts\pip install -r requirements.txt
venv\Scripts\python build.py --label "Snapshot 2026-10"
python -m http.server 8020 --directory site
```

Open http://127.0.0.1:8020. `build.py` downloads every source into `cache/` (about 2.5 GB, mostly the Plan de Uso de Terrenos shapefile; git-ignored; keep it outside a synced folder if you can) and writes the site dataset into `site/data/` (committed, so the site works from a plain static host without rebuilding). `--refresh` re-downloads.

## Method

- **Taxonomy.** Overture's `taxonomy.primary` and `hierarchy` map to VIEWPR categories (Attractions, Gastronomy, Accommodations, Visitor Services) and types via the rules in `build.py`. Places of worship are kept as their own type and excluded from counts unless toggled on.
- **Confidence.** Overture's 0–1 estimate that a listing is real and current. The map defaults to ≥ 0.50; the slider changes it. Every published statistic (`municipalities.json`, the About counts, transit access, land-use shares) uses the same baseline: confidence ≥ 0.50, places of worship excluded (`MIN_CONF` in `build.py`; 24,209 places in the 2026-10 snapshot, since homes, housing and care services were left out on 2026-10-18; 26,579 before).
- **Municipality.** Where the pin sits: point-in-polygon against the Census shoreline-clipped municipal boundaries (nearest municipal centre for the few offshore pins). The address locality is used only to block the de-duplication step, so counts agree with the polygons the map draws.
- **Duplicates.** Same municipality and category, similar normalised names (generic words and municipality names ignored), within 600 m (1,000 m for natural sites), or same phone and overlapping name within 500 m. The record with the highest confidence and most contact fields is kept; the folded count is shown in the popup.
- **Protected areas.** A place counts as inside a protected area when its point falls in a PAD-US polygon other than a proclamation boundary (`Des_Tp = PROC`), which outlines whole islands or refuges without being managed land. The strictest GAP status of the matching polygons is kept as `protected_gap`.
- **Flood exposure.** A place is "in a flood hazard area" when its point lies inside an NFHL polygon with `SFHA_TF = 'T'`. It describes the mapped hazard at the coordinates, not the building's elevation, mitigation or insurance.
- **Transit access.** Straight-line distance from each place to the nearest stop, station or terminal (ATI GTFS + OSM). "Within 400 m" is the standard quarter-mile walk. It does not account for frequency or hours; weekday departures per stop are shown in popups.
- **Sea level by year.** The 3D view maps a year and a NOAA 2022 global mean scenario (Intermediate-Low to High) to metres above the 2000 baseline, then shows the nearest whole-foot NOAA inundation layer (1–10 ft). Indicative only; local Puerto Rico values differ slightly from the global curves.
- **Transit replay.** Vehicles follow their route's line (the operator's GTFS shape, or the road network the route is snapped to) between consecutive scheduled stop times, with the time between two stops spread by distance (`trip_paths.py`; about 97% of trips, the rest keep straight stop-to-stop legs when their stops fit no line of the route). The animation shows where the timetable says vehicles are, not real-time locations. The trips files store each stop pattern's path once, with each trip's stop indices and times (format 2: see the dictionary entry "Trip record").
- **Transit supply.** `transit_supply.json` (`supply.py`, rebuilt nightly) counts the scheduled service of each stop pattern, route and stop hour by hour for a weekday, Saturday and Sunday: trips per hour, first and last departure, the average wait in four periods, run time and speed, departures and the longest wait at each stop, and island totals by hour. It drives *Service at this hour* (3D view, Time tab), the route profile (3D view, Route tab) and *Existing service near this route* in the route planner. Timetables, not observed service; only current timetables count unless the page says otherwise; públicos are in no timetable.
- **Exposure counts.** A census block is counted whole, by its internal point (the point the Census Bureau guarantees lies inside it): in a flood, sea level rise or protected area when the point is inside the polygon, near transit when it is within 400 m of a stop. Its 2020 residents and housing units are then added to its municipio, barrio and census tract, so counts add up exactly between levels. Computed, not measured; the counts of a single barrio or tract can be off where an edge cuts through blocks (`docs/exposure.md`).
- **Analytics.** Per municipality: places by category, share in SFHA, inside protected areas, within 400 m of transit, stops/stations/airports, per 1,000 residents (2020), per km² of land.

## Publishing

Live at https://observatory.foundationpr.org/ (Azure Static Web Apps, deployed by `.github/workflows/pages.yml` from `site/` on every push to `main`; see `docs/azure.md`). See `SHARE.md` for links, blurbs, the WordPress embed and the attribution line.

The `site/` folder is a complete static site with no build step. For a WordPress link, embed the published URL in an iframe or link to it from a tab.
